title: "A connected source is defined by origin, reach and destination, gated by three permission fields"
applies_when:
  - "Adding a connector, a connected source field or a media type's converter"
  - "Writing a read of passages, source entities or concepts that must apply the permission predicate"
  - "Changing how a connected source's or a document's sensitivity is narrowed, widened or published"
---

A connected source is defined by three properties:

- Its origin is the company or an external party.
- Its reach is copied or read live.
- Its destination is the passage index, the bundle, or the map.

Three permission fields gate a connected source: `published_at`, sensitivity, and the audience. One server-side predicate applies all three on every read.

The first customer's knowledge arrives in four shapes at once. Origin and reach are two axes, and together they cover all four shapes without a type for each case. Publishing is a legal act with confirmations. Sensitivity alone cannot say "not yet" or "for one group only", so `published_at` and the audience gate reads too.

Some readers skip `published_at` and apply only sensitivity and the audience:

- A concept write finds the concept it changes this way, so an owner can edit a draft.
- A person who decides an edit suggestion, an Admin or the concept's owner, reads a waiting edit on a draft this way. A draft's edits can then be decided before the concept is published. Accepting an edit does not publish the concept.
- The audit log, which only Admins see, names a draft concept to an Admin in the concept's audience.

## Rejected

- Three axes, or five flat source types. The third axis, write, has the same value for every source, so two axes are enough.
- Sensitivity as the only gate. It cannot say "indexed, not yet released".
