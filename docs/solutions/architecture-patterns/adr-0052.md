---
status: accepted
title: "A secret is stored by who owns it and what the platform does with it, never by a named class"
applies_when:
  - "Adding a secret to either tier's config module or a compose file"
  - "Adding a table or column that holds a token, a key or a sealed frame"
  - "Building a connected source's credential, an acting credential or the share agent's token"
  - "Rotating the envelope key, or deciding what a compromise of one box exposes"
---

The deployment's own secrets stay out of the database, apart from the signing keys Better Auth keeps encrypted in its own table: our own process reads them once from its environment through its tier's config module, a container outside the two tiers reads them from its own environment, a host job or a mounted service reads them from a root-only file, and what no box may hold stays in escrow. A workspace's or a person's secret never comes from the environment: it is a row the worker cannot read, under row-level security or revoked grants, sealed under the envelope key when the platform sends it onward, and hashed when the platform checks it on presentation. The seven named credential classes are retired because they mixed target systems, purposes and a lifecycle, and nothing in code could hold a list of names, while a column type, a config reader and a lint can hold a shape.

## Rejected

- One `credential` table with a `class` column: a class is a name a row carries, not a shape the schema enforces.
- `repository` and `object store` as classes: each named a target system that held secrets of several shapes.

## Consequences

- Better Auth's tables keep the library's own conventions behind ADR 0009's seam, including its JWT signing keys, which it encrypts under the auth secret.
