## Assessment A: design review of the Knowledge area

### Design-specificity verdict

The concept page is specific to this product. The title block, the mono reference, the registration marks and the masked grid make an issued drawing that only this product would use. Search and All Knowledge are competent generic lists. The guide page is a generic stacked form, and it is the weakest surface.

### Cognitive load

- Concept page: 1 failure. The title block has 10 cells (chunking), but the grid groups them well.
- Search: 0 failures.
- Guide: 4 failures. Chunking, visual hierarchy, minimal choices and progressive disclosure all fail. The corporate guide shows about 30 buttons, and every missing fact is expanded at once.
- Overall: moderate. It is low on the concept page and high on the guide.
- More than 4 options at once: each guide section, and the All Knowledge header (two filters, Columns, select-all, five sorts).

### Emotional journey

- Peak: the green "Verified by Priya Shah · 3 March 2026" is the first cell a reader sees. It gives reassurance at once.
- Valley: "Owner: No owner" and "Review by: Not set" raise doubt and offer no action.
- End: the guide ends on a long list of gaps. Its last impression is a deficit, not a next step.

### Strengths

1. The title block puts provenance and trust first and makes the concept page memorable.
2. Trust words are text with a tint, and they look the same in search, All Knowledge, guides and the title block.
3. States, keyboard support and accessible names are thorough: aria-live copy feedback, written loading states and shortcuts on every page.

### Priority issues

- **[P1] Raw footnote markup leaks into the guide.** "year.[^source-1]" shows in the Public liability insurance detail. A Bid Writer could paste it into a tender.
- **[P1] A guide section's trust contradicts its content.** "Verified automatically" sits beside "This section has no text yet" and "Expects a fact that is not yet recorded". Show no trust word on an empty section, and say what is missing in one line.
- **[P1] Empty cells in the title block are dead ends.** "No owner" and "Not set" have no action. The brief's story names "request verification", and the page has no such control.
- **[P2] The title block uses words outside the closed set.** "Status: Stable", "History: Automatically" and "Checked by" sit beside the trust word "Verified by". They repeat it in a second vocabulary.
- **[P2] Guide repetition and buttons that look like tags.** One sentence repeats on every missing fact. Tags and "Needs review" look like the Edit and Copy buttons but do nothing.

### Persona red flags

- **Bid Writer:** the footnote leak in guide detail. Search rows have no passage. The reader opens each candidate. Most Answers are Unverified, and search offers no trust filter.
- **Knowledge Curator:** the concept page has no way to set an owner or a review date, or to request verification. On All Knowledge, the checkboxes show no visible bulk action.
- **Founder-Operator:** "okf-extract/0.4" and "knowledge/sectors/…" are developer words. The useful "4 of 21 expected facts recorded" is small 13px text under the guide title.
- **Occasional Expert:** "Back to Search" repeats the breadcrumb. The breadcrumb names "Browse", which is a menu group, not a page. Lowercase "sector" and "corporate" read like raw data.

### Minor observations

- "Jump To", "Back to Search", "Add This Fact" and "New Concept" are Title Case. The brief requires sentence case.
- "Links to" repeats on every Links row.
- On mobile, the title block puts about 340px of metadata before the body.

### Questions

1. Should a guide section with no text carry any trust word at all?
2. On mobile, should the body come before the title block for a Bid Writer who came to copy?
3. Should the title block's empty cells become the place where the curator acts?
