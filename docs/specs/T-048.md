# T-048 — The people data model: groups and access requests

*Spec written 5 September 2026 by /to-spec over the T-048 ticket body, the 2026-09-05 grill's outcomes (`.scratch/readiness-2026-09-04/ui-wiring-and-t006.md` plus the owner session that cut T-048 from it), three seam decisions the owner took in this session (who a requester is, where the audit ledger is founded, what a v0.1 request may ask for), and a coordination round with the concurrent T-006 spec session (audit_event shape, group id format, implicit groups, the ADR split — agreed on both sides, recorded in Further Notes). Decisions recorded here are taken; do not re-open them.*

## Problem Statement

The platform can already say *how confidential* a piece of knowledge is — sensitivity landed with the substrate — but not *who it is for*. The glossary's *audience* names groups, and no group exists anywhere: there is no table an `audience_groups` id could resolve to, no way for an Admin to keep "the HR team" as a named set of members, and nothing for the external UI's Groups screen to ever read or write. T-006's visibility work is speccing the audience representation against group ids that nothing yet mints.

Getting into a workspace is just as one-sided. The only door is an Admin thinking to invite you: a colleague who signs in and is refused at the picker has no recorded way to ask, and an Admin has no queue of who is waiting or why. For a UK SMB where "ask IT to add me" is an email that gets lost, that is a real onboarding hole.

And underneath both: the glossary and CODING_RULES promise that every Admin act lands in *the one append-only ledger*, T-048's acts and T-006's governed write both write `audit_event` rows — and no `audit_event` table exists in the schema at all. The auth module writes structured log lines; a log line is not a ledger.

T-027 (the People screen) is blocked on all of this, and was deliberately kept a UI-wiring ticket: the tables and acts live here.

## Solution

Groups become real: flat, Entra-aligned tenant tables — a named set of members of one workspace, a person in several at once, membership changing only what a person may *see* (via audiences), never what they may *do* (their role). An Admin makes, renames, fills and deletes groups through core-slice acts; the same table shape can later carry the implicit groups a Restricted binding's named-people audience mints, so there is exactly one grouping shape in the platform.

A signed-in person who is not a member can ask to join a workspace with a sentence of why. The request lands in a queue an Admin decides: approve picks the role and mints the invitation in the same act (T-027 later emails it and ships the accept page); decline records who said no. The request surface answers the same calm acknowledgement whether the workspace named exists or not, so it can never be used to enumerate workspaces.

Every one of these acts writes the first rows of the one append-only audit ledger, founded here as an ordinary tenant table the database itself refuses to update or delete from.

No screens and no endpoints land here: these tables and acts are what `/to-tickets` places the Groups, access-requests and (later) Audit screens over, and what T-027 wires its tRPC procedures to.

## User Stories

### Groups

1. As a workspace Admin, I want to keep a named group of members ("HR team", "Sales executives"), so that a binding's audience can name one set instead of every member individually.
2. As a workspace Admin, I want a person to belong to several groups at once, so that group membership mirrors how my company actually overlaps.
3. As a workspace Admin, I want to rename a group without touching its membership or the audiences that name it, so that a team's rename is one act, not a rebuild.
4. As a workspace Admin, I want to add a member to a group and remove one from it as single acts, so that a joiner sees what their team sees the moment I act.
5. As a workspace Admin, I want to delete a group I no longer need, so that stale sets do not accumulate — accepting that anything whose audience still names it becomes *more* restricted, never less.
6. As a workspace member, I want my group memberships to end with my membership, so that leaving the workspace leaves every group with no orphan rows.
7. As an Editor or Viewer, I want group management refused to me on every verb, so that only Admins shape who sees what.
8. As a workspace member, I want my group memberships to change only what I may see, never what I may do, so that a group is never a back door to a role.
9. As the builder of T-006's read predicate, I want a caller's group ids resolvable in one lookup inside the read's own transaction, so that every visibility check pays one membership read — the Entra alignment the glossary records.
10. As a future binding-management surface, I want the group table able to carry an audience-minted (implicit) group beside the Admin-curated ones, so that a Restricted binding's named-people list becomes a group without a second grouping shape ever existing.
11. As a workspace Admin, I want to list my workspace's groups with their member counts, so that the Groups screen has its rows when it arrives.

### Access requests

12. As a signed-in person refused at the picker, I want to ask to join a workspace with a sentence of why, so that being let in does not depend on catching an Admin on the right day.
13. As the operator, I want the request surface to answer identically for a real workspace, an unknown one, and one the requester already belongs to, so that the act can never be used to enumerate workspaces or memberships.
14. As a requester, I want only one open request per workspace, so that asking twice does not spam the Admin's queue.
15. As a workspace Admin, I want waiting requests listed with who, when and why, so that the access-requests screen has its rows when it arrives.
16. As a workspace Admin, I want approving a request to pick the role (Viewer unless I say otherwise) and mint the invitation in the same act, so that approval is one decision, not two chores.
17. As a workspace Admin, I want to decline a request and have the decision recorded, so that the queue empties and the ledger says who said no.
18. As an Editor or Viewer, I want deciding access requests refused to me, so that membership stays an Admin's door.
19. As an Admin of another workspace, I want requests to a workspace I do not administer unreadable and undecidable by me, so that the tenant boundary holds per verb.
20. As the builder of T-027, I want an approved request to carry its minted invitation's id, so that the accept flow and the screens can join the two records.
21. As a workspace Admin, I want a role outside Admin, Editor and Viewer refused at approve, so that the fixed role set holds at this door as at every other.
22. As a requester whose request was approved, I want my request row to say so, so that the queue tells the truth even before the invitation email arrives (T-027's job).

### The audit ledger

23. As a workspace Admin, I want every people act on the ledger — group made, renamed, deleted; membership added, removed; access requested, approved, declined — so that the Audit screen has rows and answerability has a substrate.
24. As the builder of T-006's governed write, I want the ledger to accept an id I minted before my git commit, so that the audit id can ride in the commit's trailer.
25. As the platform, I want my own acts audited under `process:better-answers-<purpose>`, so that the reconciler and the routines are answerable under their own identity, never a person's.
26. As the erasure routine, I want the ledger's actor to be an opaque identity id, never an email or a display name, so that an append-only table never needs rewriting when a person is erased.
27. As the operator, I want `UPDATE` and `DELETE` on the ledger refused to the app's own database role, so that append-only is a database guarantee, not a code convention.

### Substrate and building

28. As a builder, I want each new table created through the RLS helper with its zero-rows proof, so that a missing scope returns no rows, never another tenant's.
29. As the operator, I want the worker's database role refused on all four tables, so that a compromised worker can neither read people data nor forge ledger rows.
30. As a builder, I want generated boundary schemas with refinements that only narrow, proved by the parity test, so that no second hand-written description of these columns exists.
31. As a reviewer, I want the ADR to state the non-adoptions — teams, plural roles, dynamic roles, temporal access — so that the next session porting the external UI does not re-open them.
32. As a reader of the glossary, I want *access request* settled in CONTEXT.md before code names it, clearly distinct from *subject request*, so that one word means one thing.
33. As the builder of T-006, I want group ids to be platform-minted ULIDs, so that the `audience_groups` boundary refinement can assume the format.

## Implementation Decisions

**Four tenant tables** — `group`, `group_member`, `access_request`, `audit_event` — every one created through the RLS helper, landed by a hand-written migration in the one journal, with generated boundary schemas entered in the registry. They are ordinary tenant tables, *not* identity-set tables: none is read before a workspace is known.

**`group`.** Platform-minted ULID id; name unique per workspace; an **origin discriminator** distinguishing Admin-curated groups from audience-minted (implicit) ones — a wide text column narrowed at the boundary to the closed pair, exactly the pattern sensitivity set. Flat: no parent reference of any kind; sub-groups would be an additive nesting migration, never a reversal (the glossary's group entry, 2026-09-05). Nothing in this ticket mints an implicit group — the discriminator exists so that when a binding-management surface does, no second grouping shape is needed, and the T-006 spec session has confirmed T-006 never mints one either.

**`group_member`.** Keyed by workspace, group and member; references the member row so that removing a member from the workspace cascades them out of every group; references the group so deleting a group deletes its memberships. Membership carries nothing about what a person may do — no role column, no permission column, by design and by the ADR.

**`access_request`.** The requester is an identity user id — a signed-in person who is not (yet) a member. A required reason. Status is one of waiting, approved, declined — no expiry state and no expiry sweeper in v0.1. One open request per workspace and requester, enforced as a partial unique index over the waiting status. Decided-by and decided-at land at decision time; approve also records the minted invitation's id on the row. There is **no asks column** — a v0.1 request asks for exactly one thing, workspace membership, so the kind is implicit; a later kind (group-join, role-change) is an additive column migration. There is **no duration column** — temporal access is Entra-PIM territory and is not adopted; the external UI's duration field is trimmed at porting time.

**`audit_event`** — the one append-only ledger, founded here. Caller-supplied text id (a ULID the writer mints), no database default, so T-006's governed write can mint the id before its git commit and carry it in the commit trailer (agreed with the T-006 spec session; the trailer decision itself belongs to T-006's ADR work). Columns say what the glossary demands — what was done (an act from the audit slice's typed event vocabulary), to what (a subject naming the record's kind and id), by whom (an actor id), when, with what confirmations (structured detail). The **actor is an opaque id** — a person by their stable identity id, the platform as `process:better-answers-<purpose>` — a deliberate divergence from the concept file's `human:<email>` convention, argued in the ADR: the email-as-credibility-signal reasoning belongs to files another OKF consumer reads, and an append-only ledger holding emails would need history rewriting on every erasure. The detail field carries ids and role words, never emails or display names. **Append-only is enforced by the database**: the migration revokes `UPDATE` and `DELETE` from the app's role on this table, and the refusal is tested per `[SEC3]`.

**The `members` slice is founded** in core (the name T-027's criteria already use), and the **audit slice gains its first real content**: the typed event vocabulary and the one write helper, per ADR 0029's slice map, called by every act inside that act's own transaction. Acts: create, rename and delete a group; add to and remove from a group; list groups; request access; approve and decline a request; list requests. Every management act takes a UserPrincipal and refuses any role but Admin; every act writes its audit event in the same transaction as its rows.

**The request-access seam.** The requester holds no Principal — a Principal requires a membership and the requester's whole point is not having one. So the act follows the platform-act precedent: the transport verifies the live session, and core performs the write under the platform principal with the person recorded as the ledger's actor. The ADR states why this is the auth-boundary case and not a hole in `[SEC2]`. The act resolves the workspace by its slug and answers **one neutral acknowledgement** in all three cases — the slug resolves, the slug does not resolve, the requester is already a member — writing a row only in the first; anti-enumeration is a functional test, and transport rate-limiting rides T-004's Postgres counters when the endpoint lands.

**The approve seam.** Approve mints the invitation row directly through the identity-write seam — the same pattern the credentials-revocation act set: a direct row write in the same transaction, no Better Auth import in core, never crossing Better Auth's endpoint path. T-004's two invitation fences therefore **stand untouched** until T-027 brings them down with the accept page: they fence Better Auth's own endpoints, which this act never calls. The invitation's exact column expectations and expiry default are read from the installed plugin's schema at build time, never assumed. Approve validates the chosen role against the three before writing; sending the email is T-027's, not this act's — an emailed invitation with no accept page would only sit pending, which is the fences' own rationale.

**Deleting a group is allowed and fail-closed.** Audience arrays carry group ids with no foreign key, so a deleted group leaves dangling ids; the read predicate's fail-closed shape means those ids resolve to no caller and the content narrows, never widens. The ADR says so; a warn-before-delete is a screen concern for the ticket that ports the Groups screen.

**`member_role_check` stands**, and gains a direct test that the database itself refuses a fourth role value.

**CONTEXT.md gains *access request***, settled before code names it, and worded to keep clear of *subject request* (a person's GDPR access-or-erasure request — an unrelated record). Proposed wording for the implementer to land: "**access request** — a signed-in person's recorded ask to join one workspace, with a reason; decided by an Admin — approved (which mints the invitation) or declined — each decision on the ledger. Not a *subject request*." The *group* entry (2026-09-05) stands unedited.

**The ADR — the people data model.** One record stating: groups flat and Entra-aligned (one membership lookup per visibility check; nesting additive, never a reversal); one grouping shape reconciling T-006's audience decisions of 2026-09-01 — `audience_groups` holds exactly these group ids, member-lists-become-groups lands as the origin discriminator's implicit kind, and the empty-intersection-forces-Restricted rule is T-006's ADR's ground, cited here and not restated; access requests as above, with the neutral-acknowledgement rule; the audit ledger's founding, its opaque actor ids and its database-enforced append-only; and the explicit non-adoption of teams (a group's *name*, never a platform word), plural roles, dynamic roles and temporal access — roles stay the three levels of ADR 0019. The ADR split with T-006 is agreed: each ADR cites the other, neither restates.

## Testing Decisions

A good test crosses the same seam a caller does and states behaviour, never implementation: for everything here that seam is the `members` slice's entry point in core's exports map, against a real Postgres via the Testcontainers harness, with state built by factories — the exact posture the workspaces slice's tests already hold. New factories for group, group membership and access request join the existing factory module.

- **Per-act behaviour** through the slice entry point: each act's served path and its refusals — Editor and Viewer refused per management verb; a foreign role refused at approve; the second open request refused; the three-way neutral acknowledgement indistinguishable to the caller.
- **Cross-workspace refusal per verb**: an Admin of workspace A cannot list, approve or decline workspace B's requests, nor touch its groups — the T-027 house pattern of one test per verb.
- **RLS zero-rows proof per new table**, picked up by the existing RLS coverage suite, whose both-ways exemption check also proves none of the four joined the identity set by accident.
- **`[SEC3]` refusal tests for every installed privilege**: the worker's role refused on all four tables; the app role's `UPDATE` and `DELETE` refused on the ledger; the direct test that `member_role_check` refuses a fourth role.
- **Boundary parity** per ADR 0028 for every generated schema and refinement (refinements only narrow), through the existing parity suite and its both-ways registry check.
- **Ledger behaviour**: an act's rows and its audit event land or fail together (the transaction is the test); a caller-supplied id is stored verbatim.

Prior art: the workspaces slice's tests and the principal tests in core, the RLS and boundary-schema suites in the schema package. This PR touches migrations and policies, so it gets the **adversarial security pass** `[SEC3]` requires beside the two document-driven review axes.

## Out of Scope

- **Screens** — blocks 3 (Groups) and 7 (access requests) are placed by `/to-tickets` over these tables; block 6 (Audit) likewise. Nothing in `apps/web` changes here.
- **Transport** — no tRPC procedures, no HTTP route; the request surface's endpoint and its rate-limit wiring land with the ticket that ships its screen (T-027's cluster).
- **Invitation email, the accept page, and the two T-004 fences** — T-027, which also owns every remaining People act.
- **The audience representation** — the audience and audience-groups columns, the CHECK, the `&&` predicate, the cascade and empty-intersection-forces-Restricted are T-006's, per the agreed ADR split.
- **Minting implicit groups** — the discriminator lands; no surface mints the implicit kind yet (binding-management territory; T-006 confirmed it never mints one either).
- **The non-adoptions** — teams as a platform concept, plural roles, dynamic roles, temporal/duration-bound access, request expiry and its sweeper.
- **T-028's operator** — nothing here touches the third principal kind or Better Auth's admin plugin.
