# T-366 — The erasure map finds the documents that name a person, and a suppression is the workspace's

*Spec written 24 September 2026 from the follow-up review of that day. T-123 declared the erasure map's documents finder "returning none until S1". S1 closed on 23/09/2026 with the finder still answering nothing, and no ticket owned it. Research for this spec found a second gap behind the first: finding a document is not enough, because the seam can only withhold a span a detector rule already raised, and a work email is never raised. The owner ruled twice on 24/09/2026. (1) A suppression is the workspace's: the seam withholds every exact occurrence of the subject's identifiers in every document of the workspace, including documents bound later, documents not yet indexed, quarantined documents, and text a switched-off rule would otherwise let through. (2) Names are matched and withheld exactly, as emails and `other` are, and the cost of withholding a namesake is accepted. Decisions recorded here are taken; do not re-open them.*

## Problem Statement

A person asks the company to erase them, and the routine runs. It rewrites the bundle's history, pseudonymises their sign-in identity and ends their membership. Every company document that names them stays searchable: `find` still returns the passage and `open` still shows their address. The documents finder answers nothing, so no suppression is written, no binding is wiped and no index run is queued. An access request says the platform holds nothing of the person in documents when it does.

Filling the finder alone would not fix this. A suppression today raises the tier of a span a detector rule already found. It never withholds text no rule raised, and the rules raise only consumer-domain email addresses, never a work address. After a wipe and a reprocess the work address would be back in the text, the next run of the routine would find it again, and the erasure would never finish. The per-document suppression also leaves gaps. It misses a document bound after the erasure, a document not yet indexed, and a quarantined one. It also misses a span withheld today by a rule the Admin later switches off.

## Solution

An erasure withholds the person's identifiers from the whole workspace for good. The routine writes one suppression per erasure request, holding the request's identifier set: its emails, its names, its other identifiers, and the addresses the person signs in with here. On every index run the seam reads the workspace's suppressions and withholds every exact occurrence of each identifier in every document's normalised text. The match is case-folded, ignores differences in whitespace and is bounded, so it never matches a longer word. It holds whatever rule is in force, whatever restore stands, and for every document the workspace binds later.

The documents finder answers where the person is named now. It reads the workspace's indexed text and names every live document whose text holds one of the identifiers. Those locations are the access answer's. They also choose which bindings the routine wipes and re-indexes immediately, rather than waiting for their next run. Once those runs finish, a second run of the routine finds nothing, wipes nothing and queues nothing.

## User Stories

### The person erased

1. As a person who asked to be erased, I want my email address, work or personal, withheld from every document the company bound, so that no search or passage shows it.
2. As a person who asked to be erased, I want my name withheld wherever it appears exactly, so that a document that names me without my address does not keep me either.
3. As a person who asked to be erased, I want a document the company binds after my erasure to have me withheld too, so that the erasure is not undone by the next upload.
4. As a person who asked to be erased, I want the text already indexed about me removed now, not at some later run, so that the erasure takes effect when the report says it does.
5. As a person who asked to be erased, I want my identifiers withheld even if an Admin later switches off the rule that first caught them, so that a settings change cannot bring me back.
6. As a person who asked to be erased, I want a span an Admin once kept in the text withheld all the same when it names me, so that an old keep cannot override my request.

### The person asking what is held

7. As a person making an access request, I want to be told which of the company's documents name me, so that the answer covers the documents and not only my sign-in records.
8. As a person making an access request, I want that answer to list where I am held and never quote a passage, so that the answer itself holds no more of me than it must.

### The Admin and the owner

9. As an Admin, I want an erasure to leave every document I bound in the platform and readable, with only the person withheld, so that the company keeps its knowledge.
10. As an Admin, I want a document I bind after an erasure to be withheld on its first run without my doing anything, so that I do not need to remember who was erased.
11. As an Admin, I want an erasure's withholdings to stay off my review screen, so that I am never asked to review, keep or dismiss a span the law requires withheld.
12. As the owner, I want the erasure report to say how many documents named the person and how many bindings are re-indexed now, with no names and no document titles, so that the report is evidence without being a new copy of the person.
13. As the owner, I want the report to say in fixed words that the identifiers are withheld from every document of the workspace, now and later, and that the original files are untouched, so that what the erasure did and did not do is plain.
14. As the owner, I want a second run of the same erasure to change nothing once the index runs it queued have finished, so that the routine stays idempotent and the replay can rely on it.
15. As the owner, I want a restore from a dump older than the erasure to re-create the suppression and re-wipe what the dump brought back, so that a restore never undoes an erasure.
16. As the owner, I want the erasure rehearsal to prove, on staging, that a bound document naming the subject by a work address and by name holds neither afterwards, so that the drill shows the erasure works on documents.

### The operator and the reviewer

17. As the operator, I want the finder to cost one index probe per identifier and a read of only the documents it hits, so that an erasure over a large workspace finishes under the dump lock in reasonable time.
18. As the operator, I want the erasure's withholding to be policy handed to the seam and outside every memo, so that an erasure reprocesses the text without re-running the detector.
19. As a reviewer, I want both tiers to agree on what counts as an occurrence, through cases they both read, so that the finder in core and the matcher in the worker cannot drift apart.
20. As a reviewer, I want the suppression's new shape, the match and the finder recorded in ADR 0020 with the rejected options, so that the next person does not re-open the per-document shape.

## Implementation Decisions

### Words first

- **suppression** changes meaning in the glossary. It becomes the workspace's entry that keeps a person's identifiers out of every derived store: one per erasure request, holding the request's identifier set, applied to every document of the workspace on every index run, now and later. The phrase "applied per document" is dropped.
- **erasure match** is added to the glossary: a bounded, case-folded occurrence of a suppression's identifier in a document's normalised text, which the seam withholds. An erasure match is a withholding and never a finding. No finding row holds it, no review reaches it and no keep releases it.
- **erasure map** is reworded. Its documents entry names the live documents whose indexed text holds an identifier. The suppression is written from the request's identifier set, not from the map. The map's documents choose which bindings are wiped now.

### The suppression is the workspace's

- A migration reshapes `suppression`. It gets one row per erasure request, keyed by workspace and erasure request. It keeps the cascade from its erasure request. The document column, its foreign key to `source_document` and its index are dropped. The check that the identifier set is shaped and not empty stands.
- The migration collapses any existing rows to one per request, since every row of a request holds the request's set. It then backfills one row for every completed erasure request whose identifier set holds an identifier and which has no row. That covers the erasures completed while the finder was a stub.
- The routine's suppression step writes the row whenever the set holds an identifier, whatever the map found, and a repeat is a no-op. The set is the request's identifiers, with the addresses the person signs in with here added to its emails. Those are the same addresses the git step already rewrites, so a member's erasure recorded by person id alone still withholds their address.
- The row is restricted personal data, as it was. The roles surface file and the boundary schema move with the table.

### The seam's erasure match

- On each run the worker reads the workspace's suppressions once and hands them, as it hands them today, to every document's withholding. This read replaces the per-document read. It lands with the table's reshape, so the worker never reads a column that has gone.
- The match runs where the officers-block rule runs, after the memoised detector and before the withholding. It is policy handed to the withholding, as ADR 0036's amendment of 21/09 requires. For every identifier it raises an erasure match at every occurrence in the normalised text. The text is normalised as `suppressed_among` normalises it, case-folded and with runs of whitespace treated as one, so an identifier broken across a line is still found. An occurrence is bounded: the characters on either side are not letters or digits, and for an email address not a character an address carries on with.
- Erasure matches are withheld in the always tier, whatever rule is in force and whatever restore stands. They compete with the run's other withheld spans by the existing overlap rule. A match that coincides with a withheld finding, or lies inside one, adds no span. A match that crosses a restored or switched-off finding is withheld all the same.
- `suppressed_among` stays as it is. A detected finding whose text is an identifier still has its tier raised, and still reports a keep the erasure overrode. The erasure match covers the occurrences no rule raised.
- `RULE_VERSION` moves, as T-220's change to the withholding moved it. The detection key does not move, because the detector reads nothing new. The findings memo and its store are untouched.

### One agreement between the tiers

- A new fixtured agreement, **erasure-match**, holds the cases both tiers read: an identifier, a text, and the occurrences it answers. The worker's matcher and core's finder each read every case. The cases cover a work address, a consumer address, a sentence-final full stop, an address followed by more address characters, a two-word name split across a line, a name inside a longer word (not a match), mixed case, and a character whose lower-case and case-folded forms differ.
- The manifest gains the agreement.

### The documents finder

- The subject the map works over carries the request's names and other identifiers as well as its emails and person id, normalised as the seam normalises them.
- For each identifier, the finder probes the workspace's indexed text through its full-text index with the identifier's words, any one of which makes a document a candidate. Only live documents count. An identifier with no indexable word makes every live document a candidate.
- It then checks each candidate document's text exactly under the erasure-match agreement. The text is the document's chunks read in order and joined. Chunks are contiguous slices, so the join is the document's indexed text, and a name split between two chunks is found. The research proposed a plain `position()` check. The agreement's boundary rule refines it, so that "Ann" never names "Annual".
- A location is a source document id. The category stays *document text*. An identifier the seam already withholds reads as withheld in the indexed text and names nothing, which is correct: there is nothing left to wipe.
- The finder reads indexed text only, inside the map's one transaction. It never reads the object store and never converts a file.

### The routine, the report and the replay

- The rest of the routine is unchanged. The bindings holding the documents the map found are wiped and re-indexed, one transaction each, and a replay wipes too.
- The report's line for documents gives three counts: documents found, bindings re-indexed now, and identifiers withheld. The old count of suppression rows is dropped. It names no document and no identifier.
- The object store sentence is replaced by one fixed sentence printed on every run: the original files in the object store are untouched; the identifiers named in this request are withheld from the text of every document in this workspace, those held now and those bound later, each time a document is indexed; and the documents found above are indexed again now. The worker rewrites a document's normalised copy on that run, so the copy the platform wrote follows the text.
- The replay copy's shape does not change. It already carries the request's identifier set and the map. A replay re-runs the routine, which writes the workspace's suppression again and wipes whatever the restored index still holds.

### The records

- ADR 0020 gets a dated amendment. It records that a suppression is the workspace's; that the seam withholds erasure matches, a withholding with no finding; that names are matched exactly and a namesake withheld; and how the finder reads the indexed text. It corrects the 20/09 amendment's "a suppression makes no finding of its own; it raises the tier of the one already there": the tier raise stands, and the erasure match now withholds what no rule raised. It records three rejected options:
  - a per-document suppression with an exact match, which misses later uploads, documents not yet indexed, quarantined documents and a rule switched off;
  - a finder in the worker that converts every original at erasure time, which breaks the map's one transaction and the access answer;
  - a keyed hash of each finding's value, which is the value.
- The `docs/adr/README.md` row for 0020 moves in the same commit.
- ADR 0036 stands, since the match is policy outside the memo.
- The following sentences are struck, each with a dated pointer to this spec:
  - T-123's "returning none until S1";
  - T-124's "no binding exists until S1, so the step is present and finds none" and its "one `suppression` per document the map found";
  - the S0 spec's step 2 "(none until S1; the family is declared and its finder returns none)", its step 6 "written for every document the map found, per document", and its step 7 "No binding exists until S1; the step is present and finds none".

## Testing Decisions

A good test here reads what an erasure leaves behind: which documents the map names, what text a document holds after a run, what the report says, and what a second run changes. It never reads a regex, a query plan or a private helper.

- **The erasure map**, in core's erasure suites. Written as a literal before the finder exists, the map for a workspace holds:
  - a document that names the subject by a work address,
  - one that names them by name split across two chunks,
  - one that names "Annual" and not "Ann",
  - one with the subject already withheld, and
  - a gone document.

  Only the first two are found. Prior art: T-123's map literals and the family exhaustiveness case.
- **The erasure-match agreement**, in both tiers. Each suite reads every case of the fixture. A case that one tier answers differently goes red in that tier's suite. Prior art: the redaction and document-chunk agreements.
- **The seam**, in the worker's redaction and landed-pipeline suites. A document naming a suppressed work address and a suppressed name is written with both withheld whatever rule is in force. A kept span that names an identifier stays withheld, and a switched-off rule does not release a match. A suppression written after a document's first run withholds on the next run. Prior art: the delivery-note suppression case and the one-name suppression case.
- **The routine**, in core's erasure routine suite:
  - the suppression is one row per request, and is written for a request by person id alone;
  - the report's documents line carries the three counts, and the fixed sentence is printed on every arm;
  - the found documents' bindings are wiped and re-indexed.
- **Idempotency, end to end**, in the document-shaped cross-tier test, S1's seam, where the Admin's acts run over real Postgres and a real object store and the worker runs as a real process. Its case is *a second run of the erasure over the same request, after the worker's run, finds no document, wipes nothing and queues no index run*. The same case holds that `find` returns no passage naming the subject and that the document's normalised copy holds neither identifier.
- **The replay**, in core's erasure replay suite. A restore from a dump older than the request re-creates the workspace's suppression and wipes the bindings the restored index names.
- **The migration**, in the schema suite. Rows collapse to one per request, completed erasures are backfilled, and the roles surface reads back as the file says.
- **The rehearsal**, in the ops suite and on staging. The `erasure-rehearsal` seed gains a bound document naming the subject by a work address and by name. Its dump check covers the chunk table.

## Out of Scope

- Names inside concept bodies. They stay listed by IRI for the owner to edit, as S0 decided.
- The original files in the object store. They stay untouched, as S0 decided; only what the platform wrote from them is withheld.
- A floor on how short an identifier may be. The owner accepted withholding a namesake. Whether a very short identifier should be refused when the request is recorded is a question below, not a decision here.
- Showing erasure matches on the Sources screen. They are not findings, and nothing asks for them there.
- What happens to a document's findings when its content hash moves. That stays S4's, as ADR 0020's 20/09 amendment left it.

### Questions only the owner can answer

- **Erasures already completed.** The backfill writes their suppression, so every later run withholds. Should they also be re-run through the routine once this lands, for example by `replay-erasures`, so their already-indexed text is wiped now rather than at each binding's next run?
- **A member's display name.** T-355 gives every person a display name. Should the suppression add it to the names automatically, as the sign-in addresses are added to the emails? The default here is no. A first name alone would withhold every occurrence of it in the workspace.
- **A floor on identifier length.** A two-letter name, or a common word recorded as an identifier, is withheld wherever it stands alone. Should the request refuse an identifier shorter than some length when it is recorded?
- **The seams.** The seams proposed above are: core's erasure map, the worker's seam suites, the erasure-match agreement, the document-shaped cross-tier test and the rehearsal. They are the existing ones plus one agreement.
