# T-311 — The toolbar seam: the open tab and a view's act state in `shared/`

## Problem Statement

T-227 landed the toolbar filled from the open view's route: `apps/web/src/app/router.tsx` carries a
`ViewToolbar` on each view route's `staticData` — `tabs` as id and name, `acts` as a `ReactNode` —
and `apps/web/src/app/frame.tsx:42` reads it off the deepest match. Static route data was the right
call and stays: it is built once, so a view that throws leaves its tab list standing and a selected
tab always controls a panel. The alternative failed on the record — axe's `aria-valid-attr-value`,
critical, on a tab whose `aria-controls` pointed at a panel the thrown view had taken with it, which
is the failure `apps/web/e2e/failed-screen.spec.ts:67` now holds down.

Two consequences of *where the shared state lives* bite the next screen, T-136's Sources.

- **An act cannot see its view's state.** T-136's review needs two bulk acts as select-then-command
  over finding groups a reader has ticked. The act sits in the toolbar; the ticking happens in the
  view. Nothing joins them.
- **A feature view may not read the open tab.** `useOpenTab` is a context in the app zone
  (`apps/web/src/app/toolbar.tsx:21`). The web's three zones import app → features → shared and never
  back, so a view under `apps/web/src/features/` cannot import it. That has not bitten yet only because the
  region's one use, `apps/web/src/app/views/routes-and-spend-view.tsx`, is itself in the app zone.
  T-136's view is not.

The owner also asked, on the same reading, that the landed shell be reviewed against
`apps/web/.claude/skills/writing-react-effects/SKILL.md`: no `useEffect` that derives state, handles
an event or synchronises props, and a listener answered by `useSyncExternalStore` rather than an
effect.

## Solution

The seam is in the right place. What is misplaced is the state.

A view's tabs and acts keep reaching the shell exactly as they do now, as typed data on the route.
What moves is the small amount of live state a view and its acts must both see: the open tab, and a
slot a view writes and its acts read. Both go to `apps/web/src/shared/view-toolbar.tsx`, which both
zones may import. The shell keeps everything it owns today — the region, the tab list, the keyboard
behaviour and the panel — and the guarantee that a broken view leaves the bar standing is untouched,
because the tabs are still static data the view is not asked for at paint time.

Nothing a reader sees changes. The second half of the work is the effects sweep of the shell as
landed, which found one rewrite.

## User Stories

### Commanding over what I picked

As an Admin reviewing a binding's findings, I tick several finding groups in the list and press
*Narrow these documents* in the bar above it, and the act runs over what I ticked. The act is inert
while I have ticked nothing, so I am never offered a command with no object.

### Coming back to a screen that broke

As an Admin whose view failed to draw, the tabs above it are still there and still say which one is
open, one of them is a way back in, and when the view draws again no act is holding a selection from
before the failure.

### Building a screen

As the agent building Sources, I declare my view's tabs and acts as data on my route the way T-227
asks, and I read the open tab and my own act state from `shared/`. I import nothing from
`apps/web/src/app/`, and lint says so rather than a reviewer.

### Reading the shell

As the next person in `apps/web/src/app/`, every piece of state in the shell is either derived during
render or read from a store through `useSyncExternalStore`, and the one redirect that used to run in
an effect now runs where the router's other redirects already run.

## Implementation Decisions

### Why (b), and what the other two shapes could not do

Three shapes were weighed against the landed code.

- **(a) The context moves to `shared/` and the route element hands the view a prop.** Cheap —
  `componentFor` in `apps/web/src/app/router.tsx:68` returns a zero-argument element factory, so
  wrapping it is a few lines. It answers the open tab and nothing else: an act still cannot see the
  view's state, so T-136 would push both bulk acts into the content and contradict its own text. Half
  an answer, rejected for that.
- **(b) A shared module holding the open tab and a view-state slot, which the view writes and its
  declared acts read.** Chosen. The tab list stays static, so the thrown-view guarantee and the panel
  contract are untouched by construction. `Toolbar`, `ViewTabsRoot` and `ViewPanel` keep the
  signatures they have, so the seven tests in `apps/web/test/toolbar.test.tsx` and every toolbar
  assertion in the browser suite stand.
- **(c) The view renders its toolbar content into the region through a slot or a portal.** Rejected on
  recorded evidence. Portalled tabs go when the view goes, leaving a selected tab whose
  `aria-controls` names nothing, which is the exact `aria-valid-attr-value` critical failure that
  decided T-227. It breaks `apps/web/e2e/failed-screen.spec.ts:67` outright and puts a wait into the
  aria snapshot at `apps/web/e2e/frame.spec.ts:321`, because the region is empty on the first paint.

The insight that makes (b) small: static route data freezes the *element tree*, not the hooks inside
it. An act declared as `acts: <NarrowSelected />` is already live on every render. It only needs
somewhere to read from that a feature is allowed to import.

### What moves

`apps/web/src/shared/view-toolbar.tsx` is new and holds the `ViewTab` and `ViewToolbar` types,
`isFilled`, the open-tab context with its `useOpenTab` reader, and the view-state slot. The types
move with the rest because a feature view must name `ViewToolbar` to declare one, and a type in the
app zone is as unreachable as a hook.

`apps/web/src/app/toolbar.tsx` keeps `Toolbar`, `ViewTabsRoot` and `ViewPanel` — the region, the tab
list and the panel are the shell's and stay in the app zone. It imports what it needs from `shared/`
and re-exports nothing, so there is one name for each thing.

`apps/web/src/app/router.tsx` and `apps/web/src/app/frame.tsx` change their import of the type and
of `isFilled`, and the `StaticDataRouteOption` declaration at `apps/web/src/app/router.tsx:122` keeps
its one field. `apps/web/src/app/views/routes-and-spend-view.tsx` reads `useOpenTab` from `shared/`.

### The act channel

One slot, owned by the open view, written by the view and read by its acts. The shell mounts it
inside `ViewTabsRoot`, so it spans the toolbar and the panel and both halves see the same value.

The slot is untyped where the shell holds it and typed where a view uses it: a feature declares one
helper of its own — for T-136, a `useReviewSelection` over its finding groups — and its table and its
act components both call that helper. Type agreement is the feature's own business, stated once. The
shared surface stays two names wide, which is the whole point of putting it there.

The slot is **derived, not reset**, the way `ViewTabsRoot` already derives the open tab from a pick
that may belong to another view. It carries the path of the view that wrote it, and a read from a
different view answers empty. So navigation empties the slot during render, with no effect and no
second source of truth.

### The thrown view and the panel contract

An act lives outside the panel, so it survives a view that threw, and the selection the view wrote
survives with it. Two rules settle it. An act is inert on an empty slot, which is a property of the
act and costs nothing. And a tab switch remounts the panel — `ViewPanel` already keys `TabsContent`
by the open tab — which is the only way back into a thrown view and empties the slot on the way. So
after a view throws, the way back in is the way `failed-screen.spec.ts` already drives it, and the
acts are inert when the reader arrives.

Within the single render in which a view throws, an act may still show the count the view wrote
before it failed. That is accepted and named here rather than solved with an effect.

**What the build found, T-313.** The slot is mounted in `ViewTabsRoot`, above the panel, so
remounting the panel cannot empty state held over it: the sentence above is wrong about the
mechanism and right about the behaviour. The slot is emptied during render instead, by the open
tab it was written under having changed — React's own adjustment of state that follows a value,
and still no effect and no second source of truth. Keying the slot by the open tab, the obvious
alternative, was rejected: the key would remount the children, the tab list among them, and the
arrow keys would lose the trigger holding focus.

### Effects: the sweep of the shell as landed

Read against the skill, five files, one rewrite.

- **`apps/web/src/app/frame.tsx:26` — rewrite.** A `useEffect` watches a membership refusal and calls
  `navigate` with a replace. It is an event handled in an effect, and the router already has the
  place for it: `indexRoute` and every screen route in `apps/web/src/app/router.tsx` throw `redirect`
  from `beforeLoad`. The refusal redirect moves to `shellRoute`'s `beforeLoad`, throwing `redirect`
  the way its neighbours do, and the effect goes with the `here` and `navigate` reads that serve only
  it. The cost is real and stated: membership is fetched by `useMembership` inside the component, so
  `beforeLoad` must reach the same data through the query client's `ensureQueryData` rather than
  refetching. The two sign-in and picker paths the effect skips today become the guard on the new
  redirect, and the address carried in `redirect=` is carried the same way.
- **`apps/web/src/app/wide-layout.ts:20` — already right.** The resize listener is
  `useSyncExternalStore` over a `listen`/`wideNow` pair, with the breakpoint read from the stylesheet
  so the number is written once. No change.
- **`apps/web/src/app/secondary-nav-showing.ts:26` — no effect, no change.** Storage is read once in a
  lazy `useState` initialiser and written in the setter. A `storage` event listener would make two
  tabs agree, and that is deliberately not done: the spec calls the choice one browser's own, and
  adding it is behaviour this ticket was not given.
- **`apps/web/src/app/navigation-control.tsx` — no effect.** Sheet state is `useState` driven by
  `onOpenChange`.
- **`apps/web/src/app/toolbar.tsx` — no effect, and the pattern to keep.** `ViewTabsRoot` derives the
  open tab during render from a pick that may not belong to this view, and `ViewPanel` keys the panel
  rather than resetting it. The new slot in `shared/` copies both.

### Layering

Unchanged in direction and now enforceable. `apps/web/src/app/` composes, a view declares its tabs
and acts, `shared/` is the ground both stand on. A feature view imports `@/shared/view-toolbar.tsx`
and never `@/app/toolbar.tsx`, and `apps/web/test/lint-rules.test.ts` is where that is held rather
than in a reviewer's head.

### The two amendments to `docs/specs/T-225.md`

They land in the implementing commit, not with this spec.

- ***Layering***. The bullet ends "the way a view reaches the toolbar is props passed down through
  the route, never a feature importing the shell". It gains the other half: shell state a view must
  read — the open tab, and the slot its acts read — lives in `shared/`, which both zones may import,
  so the direction holds without a prop for state the shell does not own.
- ***The regions***, the **Toolbar** bullet. "what goes in it is the view's decision" gains what an
  act may see: an act carries its own behaviour and reads the view's own state from `shared/`, the
  declaration on the route staying data built once.

### What T-136 writes against it

Its review view declares `SOURCES_REVIEW_TOOLBAR` in its own feature directory — tabs by name, acts
as elements of its own components — and its route carries it as `staticData` the way
`ROUTES_AND_SPEND_TOOLBAR` does today. Its act components and its findings table both call one
`useReviewSelection` helper over the shared slot, so *narrow these documents* is commanded over the
groups a reader ticked, and the screen still never holds a finding id. It reads the open tab from
`shared/` and imports nothing from `apps/web/src/app/`.

## Testing Decisions

The point of choosing (b) is that the held behaviour is already held. These stay, unchanged but for
one import path, and each is the reason a shape was rejected as much as a test:

- `apps/web/test/toolbar.test.tsx`, all seven — the open view's tabs in its own order with the open
  one selected; the tab a reader picks and the words for the one nobody built; another view opening on
  its own first tab; the open tab's panel and the view inside it; a view with neither tabs nor acts
  getting no toolbar and no panel; tabs at the start and acts at the end; acts alone with no tabs.
- `apps/web/e2e/frame.spec.ts`, the toolbar block — the tabs with their aria snapshot and arrow keys,
  the region's place between the top bar and the content, a view with no toolbar, the keyboard order,
  and the secondary nav's button with the toolbar taking the freed width.
- `apps/web/e2e/failed-screen.spec.ts` — a view that throws leaves the three regions standing, the
  toolbar with it, and its other tab is a way back in.

Two are added, both in `apps/web/test/toolbar.test.tsx`:

- **An act reads what its view wrote.** The tabbed view already declares an act; the act reads the
  slot, the view writes a selection, and the act says what it will run over.
- **An act over a thrown view is inert.** The view throws holding a selection, the tabs stand, the
  reader goes to the other tab and back, and the act is inert because the slot is empty.

`apps/web/test/lint-rules.test.ts` gains the pair of cases that make the layering real: a feature may
import `@/shared/view-toolbar.tsx`, and a feature importing `@/app/toolbar.tsx` fails lint.

The effects rewrite is held by what already holds the redirect: the sign-in and picker paths in the
browser suite, which must pass unchanged, since a reader cannot tell a `beforeLoad` redirect from an
effect's.

## Out of Scope

- `?tab=` in the address. T-227's review cut it as scope the spec never granted and this does not
  grant it either, so a deep link still lands on a view's first tab.
- Moving `apps/web/src/app/views/routes-and-spend-view.tsx` into a feature. It stays where it is; the
  seam is proved by lint and by T-136's view, not by a move with no reader behind it.
- A visible act on the *routes and spend* view. Nothing lands unused, and the slot is proved in the
  test rather than by inventing a button the spec never asked for.
- `apps/web/src/features/auth/choose-workspace-screen.tsx:72`, which redirects in an effect the same
  way `Frame` does. Seen, not this ticket's: it is a feature's own screen and not the shell.
- Effects in `apps/web/src/shared/ui/`. Those files come from the registries under ADR 0033 and are
  not rewritten here.
- Making two tabs agree on whether the secondary nav is showing.

## Further Notes

Two tickets, both cut from this spec, and they run in parallel: the seam and the effects sweep touch
different concerns and mostly different files. They meet in `apps/web/src/app/router.tsx` — the seam
changes an import, the sweep adds a `beforeLoad` — and in `apps/web/src/app/frame.tsx`, so the second
of the two to land merges `main` first, which is the arrangement T-227 and T-228 already ran.

ADR 0033 (the registries), ADR 0037 (the budgets, which a tab switch does not bind). The rules that
bind the work are in `CODING_RULES.md` and `apps/web/CODING_RULES.md`: read the web's three zones and
the direction app → features → shared, which is the rule that put this ticket on the board, and its
rule on a screen that throws leaving the frame standing, beside the constitution's rules on an act's
outcome and on the accessibility gate. Prior art: T-227 on `d9463943` and T-228 on
`d5076341`, and T-227's own Progress note, which named this seam as seen and not its.

T-136 waits on the seam ticket and not on the sweep.
