# T-225 — The three-region workspace shell

*Spec written 21 September 2026 from the owner's ruling of that day, recorded on T-136: the app shell is a three-region workspace shell — an icon rail, a secondary nav that toggles open and closed and swaps per screen, and a toolbar with tabs. It is a ticket of its own, cut ahead of T-136 (the Sources screen). The owner confirmed the test seams, the wording (*per screen*, with *view* as the one new word) and the scope of the secondary nav's content the same day. The ruling was weighed against the three tests for a decision record — hard to reverse, surprising without context, a real trade-off — and is not one: this spec, the glossary and T-136 are the record. Decisions recorded here are taken; do not re-open them.*

## Problem Statement

Control Centre's shell is a flat list of its six screens beside one column of content. That was the right size for the first screen. It is the wrong shape for what comes next: each screen is several things — Sources is bindings, gates, a plan, backlogs; System is signals, health, routes and spend, backups — and a flat list gives a person nowhere to see what a screen holds, no way to move between its parts, and no consistent place for the tabs and acts that belong to the part they are looking at. The first real screen, Sources, is about to be built, and every screen after it binds the way that one does. Built inside the flat shell, each would invent its own inner navigation and its own toolbar, and the shell would be rebuilt under all of them later.

## Solution

The shell becomes three regions around the screen's content. Down the left edge, an **icon rail** lists Control Centre's six screens. Beside it, a **secondary nav** lists the open screen's **views** — the parts the glossary already names for that screen — and swaps when the person moves to another screen; a button opens and closes it, and the choice is remembered. Above the content, a **toolbar** carries the open view's tabs on one side and its acts on the other, filled by the view and absent when the view has neither. A top bar names the workspace, says where the person is, and holds who they are, their role and the way to sign out.

The reference shape is the workspace shell on branch `arena/01a0833b-better-answers` and the three screenshots under `.scratch/ui-inspiration/` — two of the shell, with the secondary nav open and closed, and one showing only the kind of thing a screen's secondary nav holds. They are read, never merged: the components come from the registries, the values from the tokens, and the words from the glossary.

## User Stories

### Finding a screen

1. As a workspace member, I want an icon rail that lists Control Centre's six screens in the glossary's order, so that the product's shape is one glance.
2. As a workspace member, I want each rail entry to carry its screen's name — as its accessible name and as a tooltip on hover and on focus — so that an icon is never the only thing telling me where it leads.
3. As a workspace member, I want the rail to mark the screen I am on, in a way that does not rest on colour alone, so that I always know where I am.
4. As a workspace member, I want the rail to stay where it is when the secondary nav is closed, so that moving between screens never costs me a step.

### Finding a view

5. As a workspace member, I want the secondary nav to list the open screen's views, named in the glossary's words for that screen, so that I can see what a screen holds before I go looking.
6. As a workspace member, I want the secondary nav to swap to the new screen's views when I change screen, so that it never shows me another screen's parts.
7. As a workspace member, I want a view that is not built yet to be listed and to say so in words when I open it, so that the product's shape is visible and nothing pretends.
8. As a workspace member, I want each view to have an address of its own, so that I can bookmark it, share it and come back to it after signing in.
9. As a workspace member, I want a screen's own address to land me on that screen's default view, and the addresses I already use to keep working, so that nothing I have saved breaks.
10. As a workspace member, I want the secondary nav to mark the view I am on, so that I know which part of the screen I am reading.

### Making room

11. As a workspace member, I want one button that closes the secondary nav and opens it again, so that a wide table can have the width when I need it.
12. As a workspace member, I want the shell to remember whether I left the secondary nav open or closed, on this browser, so that I do not set it again on every visit.
13. As a workspace member, I want the toolbar and the content to take the freed width when the secondary nav is closed, so that closing it is worth doing.

### Working in a view

14. As a workspace member, I want the open view's tabs in one consistent place above its content, so that every screen's inner divisions work the same way.
15. As a workspace member, I want the open view's acts — the things I can do to what I am looking at — at the other end of the same toolbar, so that I never hunt for them.
16. As a workspace member, I want no toolbar at all on a view that has neither tabs nor acts, so that an empty bar never sits above the content.
17. As a workspace member, I want the top bar to say which screen and view I am in, so that a deep link tells me where it landed me.

### Knowing who and where I am

18. As a workspace member, I want the top bar to name the workspace I am in before anything else, so that I never act in the wrong one.
19. As a workspace member, I want my name, my role and the way to sign out in the top bar, so that I can see who the platform thinks I am.
20. As a workspace member, I want the shell to show nothing about me until it knows it, so that it never says something it cannot know.
21. As a person who is not signed in, has not picked a workspace, or whose membership has ended, I want to be sent where the shell sends me today — to sign in with my address carried, or to the picker — so that the new shell changes nothing about who gets in.

### Small screens, keyboards and assistive technology

22. As a person on a narrow screen, I want the rail and the secondary nav behind one menu button that opens them over the content, so that the content keeps the width and nothing scrolls sideways at 320 pixels.
23. As a keyboard user, I want the skip link first, then the rail, the secondary nav, the top bar, the toolbar and the content in that order, each control operable and its focus ring visible, so that I can use the shell without a pointer.
24. As a keyboard user, I want the skip link to move focus into the content, so that my next tab stop is in the view and not back in the navigation.
25. As a screen-reader user, I want the rail, the secondary nav and the content to be separate labelled landmarks, the open-and-close button to say whether the nav is open, and the current screen and view to be announced as current, so that the structure I hear is the structure a sighted person sees.
26. As a screen-reader user, I want the navigation that opens over the content on a narrow screen to take focus when it opens, hold it while it is open and give it back when it closes, so that I am never left behind it.
27. As a workspace member, I want a view that fails to leave all three regions standing and say what happened, so that one broken view never takes the navigation with it.

### Building and reviewing

28. As a developer building a screen, I want each region to take what it shows and what it calls back as typed props and to fetch nothing itself, so that a screen composes the shell and the shell never knows a screen's data.
29. As a developer adding a view, I want one list to be where a view is declared — its screen, its name, its address, whether it is built — and the rail, the secondary nav and the router all to be made from it, so that a view cannot be in the navigation and missing from the router, or the other way round.
30. As a reviewer, I want every component in the shell to come from a registry and every colour, size and radius to be a token, so that the shell looks like the product and not like its reference.

## Implementation Decisions

### Words first

- **View** is settled in the glossary before it appears in code: one of the parts a screen of Control Centre is divided into, listed in the secondary nav, each with an address of its own. The glossary's entry for Control Centre already lists each screen's parts; those are the views, in that order and in those words.
- The regions are named in the glossary's *Platform surfaces* beside Control Centre: **icon rail**, **secondary nav**, **toolbar**, **top bar**. *Section* stays a guide's node and is never used for any of them — the reference branch's "section nav" is the secondary nav here.
- A tab is a tab: a division inside one view, the view's own business, and not a glossary word.

### One list

- The list that declares the six screens grows to declare each screen's views: name, address, and whether it is built. The rail, the secondary nav and the router are all made from it. A screen names its default view; the screen's own address leads there.
- Today's addresses keep their meaning. The System screen's address still reaches the routes list, which becomes System's *routes and spend* view and that screen's default while it is the only view built.
- An unbuilt view is a real destination that says in words that it is not built yet, as the five unbuilt screens do today.

### The regions

- **Icon rail** — the six screens, each an icon with its name as the accessible name and as a tooltip. Always present above the medium breakpoint.
- **Secondary nav** — the open screen's views, under the screen's name. Opened and closed by one button that says which state it is in. The choice is kept in the browser's own storage — a single open-or-closed value, nothing about the person, nothing sent to the server.
- **Top bar** — the workspace's name first; then where the person is, as screen and view; then the person's name and role with sign-out in a menu. Nothing about the person is shown until membership is known.
- **Toolbar** — filled by the open view through typed props: tabs at the start, acts at the end. A view that declares neither gets no toolbar. The shell owns the region and its keyboard behaviour; what goes in it is the view's decision. This ticket gives it one real use so the region is proved and nothing lands unused: System's *routes and spend* view carries two tabs, *Routes* — the list that exists — and *Spend*, which says it is not built.
- **Below the medium breakpoint** the rail and the secondary nav live behind one menu button and open over the content in a sheet that traps and returns focus. The content reflows at 320 pixels with no sideways scroll.
- The membership check, the redirect to sign-in with the address carried, the redirect to the picker, and the guard against folding an address into itself all move across unchanged. The shell still fetches membership and nothing else.

### Built from the registries

- Every component comes from a registry already in use (ADR 0033); the items the reference branch names as missing from the installed set — sheet, tooltip, separator, scroll-area, toggle-group — are installed as they are needed and no sooner.
- The icon set is the one the design skill names. It is not yet a dependency of the web app; this ticket adds it, pinned to the version read from its source on the day. T-136's note that it would add it is superseded.
- A colour, a size or a radius that is not a token is a defect. The reference branch writes six such values; none crosses.
- The reference branch's typed-props, no-fetch discipline and its landmark structure are what is taken from it. Its view model — list, kanban, calendar, dashboard — its sample data and its preview entry points are not this product's and are not taken.

### Layering

- The shell belongs to the app zone and composes shared components; a screen's view belongs to its feature and hands the shell its tabs and acts. The direction app → features → shared holds, so the way a view reaches the toolbar is props passed down through the route, never a feature importing the shell.

## Testing Decisions

A good test here reads what a person sees and does — a landmark, a name, a current marker, where focus is, what an address shows — and never a class name, a component's internals or the storage key.

- **The browser suite is the deliverable seam.** The shell's spec is rewritten for the three regions: the rail reaches each of the six screens; the secondary nav lists the open screen's views and swaps on a change of screen; an unbuilt view says so; the button closes and opens the nav, says its state, and the choice survives a reload; the toolbar shows System's two tabs and is absent on a view with neither tabs nor acts; the keyboard order is the one story 23 gives and the skip link moves focus; at a narrow viewport the menu button opens the navigation over the content, focus is held and returned, and nothing scrolls sideways at 320 pixels. The redirect cases it holds today — no session, no workspace picked, the address carried — stay as they are.
- **The routes spec** keeps its meaning: the routes list is reached by the address it has today, and a second workspace's routes are never on the page.
- **The accessibility gate** runs over the shell with the secondary nav open, with it closed, and with the narrow-screen navigation open.
- **The failed-screen specs**, in the browser and in the component suite, hold that a view that throws leaves the rail, the secondary nav and the top bar standing.
- **The component suite** holds the one-list rule in both directions: every view on the list has a route and a nav entry, and every route under a screen is a view on the list. It drives the router with a memory history, as it does today.
- Prior art: the shell, routes, failed-screen and accessibility-gate specs in the browser suite; the shell and failed-screen tests in the component suite. `/browser-suite` is the skill for the first; `/better-answers-design` for anything a person will look at.
- No new seam, and no change to the api or to `packages/core`.

## Out of Scope

- Any view's content beyond what exists: the routes list is the only built view, and this ticket builds no other. The Sources screen's views are T-136's.
- Search, a create button, reports, notifications, an avatar image, badges on nav entries and a workspace switcher in the top bar — all in the screenshots, none in this ticket. Changing workspace stays the picker's job.
- Collapsible groups, counts and nested entries inside the secondary nav. The third screenshot shows them as the kind of thing a screen's nav may grow; a screen that needs one brings it in its own ticket.
- A keyboard shortcut for the open-and-close button.
- The reader surface. The route spec names the same reference shape for it; it is its own block's decision.
- Remembering the open-or-closed choice across browsers or devices.
