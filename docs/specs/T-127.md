### Wave A — the descriptors and the row (**TDD**)

1. `packages/schema/src/job-tables.ts`: one declared descriptor per kind — `JOB_KIND_DESCRIPTORS`, each record carrying `kind`, the claiming tier, whether the row names a subject, the reasons it may carry (empty when it carries none), and the role that may enqueue it. `JOB_KINDS` derives from the list; `NIGHTLY_AUDIT_KIND`, `FULL_REBUILD_KIND` and `REBUILD_REASONS` stand. `INDEX_KIND = "index"` and `INDEX_REASONS = ["bound", "restored", "rule-change", "wiped", "narrowed"]` are the third record.
2. `job` gains `subjectId: text("subject_id")`, nullable.
3. The three CHECKs are **derived from the descriptor list**, not hand-written:
   - `job_kind_check` — `kind IN (…)` from the descriptors' kinds.
   - `job_subject_check` — `(subject_id IS NOT NULL) = (kind IN (<kinds whose descriptor requires a subject>))`, a biconditional so it refuses both ways.
   - `job_reason_check` — `(reason IS NOT NULL) = (kind IN (<kinds with reasons>)) AND (reason IS NULL OR (kind, reason) IN (<the descriptors' (kind, reason) pairs>))`. The pair list is what stops coupling a reason to one kind while still refusing an `index` reason on a rebuild. A rebuild's six reasons stand.
4. The **run key** as a partial unique index: `uniqueIndex("job_queued_subject_key").on(workspaceId, kind, subjectId).where(sql\`status = 'queued'\`)`. NULL `subject_id` is distinct in Postgres, so the audit and the rebuild are untouched — no `NULLS NOT DISTINCT`.
