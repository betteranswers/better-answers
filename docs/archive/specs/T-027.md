# T-027 — The People screen: members, roles, invitations, requests, groups and the audit log, every act the members slice's over tRPC

*Spec written 25 September 2026 from the owner's grill of 24/09/2026 over T-027 and T-028 (eighteen decisions over five rounds, reviewed the same day by an independent architecture pass), the amendments it wrote to ADRs 0009, 0014, 0017, 0020, 0038 and 0043, the glossary as that grill left it, T-027's lines and decisions of 02/09 and 05/09, T-063's and T-048's specs, T-355, and the route's P1 block. It covers T-027's part of P1; P1's other lines stay on the route for their own spec (Out of Scope).*

## Problem Statement

A workspace Admin cannot manage the people in their workspace. Nothing lists the members; nothing changes a role, invites, removes or revokes; the invitation endpoints are fenced with a *not implemented* answer; access requests are recorded and approved in core but reach no screen and send no email. The first client's people were made members by an ops command because there was no other way.

Beneath that, the organisation plugin mounts write endpoints an Admin can reach with a raw request — invite, change a role, remove — none of which writes an audit event, so an act could change who holds a workspace and leave no record. One of its reads, the slug check, tells any signed-in stranger whether a company is a customer, which the access request's neutral answer exists to prevent. And once a checker is removed, *Checked by* on the concepts they checked shows a raw id, because the name is read through the membership that no longer exists.

## Solution

People becomes a working screen of Control Centre with three built views:

- **Members** — the screen's default view, with the tabs *Members*, *Invitations* and *Requests*. An Admin sees every member with their role and groups, opens one, changes their role, removes them or revokes their credentials in this workspace; invites a person by address with a role, resends or cancels a waiting invitation; approves or declines an access request.
- **Groups** — the groups list with member counts, over the group acts already in core.
- **Audit log** — every audit event in this workspace, newest first, filterable by family.

Every act is the members slice's, reached over tRPC, keyed by (workspace, person id), and writes its audit event in the same transaction as the change. Accepting an invitation is the members slice's act too: the person follows the link, signs in with that address, gives a display name if they have none, and accepts on the SPA's accept page. The organisation plugin keeps its tables and its reads and loses its writes, and the slug check goes with them, so the members slice is the only door. A workspace always keeps an Admin. An Admin who finds an inappropriate display name flags it to the operator rather than correcting it. *Checked by* reads the checker's name from their person row, so it stands after they leave.

## User Stories

### Members

1. As an Admin, I want to see every member of my workspace with their display name, address, role and groups, so that I know who can read and change our knowledge.
2. As an Admin, I want the member list to be the People screen's first view, so that the thing I come to People for is what I land on.
3. As an Admin, I want to search the member list by name or address, so that I can find one person in a workspace of fifty.
4. As an Admin, I want to open a member and see their role, groups, when they joined and whether their credentials here were revoked, so that I can act on one person with the facts in front of me.
5. As an Editor or a Viewer, I want People to be out of my reach, so that managing people stays with the workspace's Admins.
6. As an Admin, I want the list to name every person by their display name, so that I see the name they gave and not an id.

### Roles

7. As an Admin, I want to change a member's role to Admin, Editor or Viewer, so that what they may do matches their job.
8. As an Admin, I want each role's one-line meaning shown where I choose it, so that I pick the right level without reading documentation.
9. As an Admin, I want a role outside the three refused, so that no fourth level creeps in.
10. As a member whose role changed, I want the change to apply on my next action, so that a demotion takes effect at once.
11. As an Admin, I want every role change on the audit log with its old and new role, so that anyone can see who changed whose access and when.

### The last Admin

12. As an Admin, I want demoting or removing the last Admin refused with a word that tells me to promote someone else first, so that my workspace is never left with nobody able to manage it.
13. As one of two Admins, I want to demote or remove myself while the other remains, so that I can hand the workspace over.
14. As one of two Admins, I want two removals made at the same moment to leave the workspace with an Admin, so that a race never locks us out.
15. As an Admin whose act lost a race, I want a *try again* answer rather than a failure, so that I read the list again and decide again.

### Removal

16. As an Admin, I want to remove a member, so that someone who has left the company loses access.
17. As an Admin, I want removal to end the person's access in this workspace on every session and client they hold, not only the one they are using, so that removal is complete.
18. As a removed person who belongs to another workspace, I want my access there untouched, so that one company's decision never reaches another's.
19. As an Admin, I want removal on the audit log with me as actor and the person as subject, so that the record of the membership outlives the membership.
20. As an Admin, I want a removed person to leave every group in the workspace with the membership, so that no group still counts them.
21. As an Admin, I want *Checked by* on the concepts a removed member checked to keep their name, so that the history of who vouched for what stays readable.

### Revoking credentials in this workspace

22. As an Admin, I want to revoke a member's credentials in this workspace, so that a lost laptop or a leaked token stops working here at once without removing the person.
23. As an Admin, I want revocation to end every session and every token the person holds for this workspace, so that nothing issued before it still works here.
24. As a person whose credentials were revoked, I want a fresh sign-in to work again, so that revocation ends what was issued and does not bar me.
25. As an Admin, I want revocation never to tell me whether the person belongs to any other workspace, so that I learn nothing about other companies.
26. As an Admin, I want revocation on the audit log, so that the response to an incident is on record.

### Invitations

27. As an Admin, I want to invite a person by email address with a role, so that a new colleague can join.
28. As an Admin, I want the invitation emailed to the address with a link to accept, so that the person needs nothing but their inbox.
29. As an Admin, I want the answer to an invitation to be the same whether or not the address already uses Better Answers, so that I cannot learn who else is a customer's user.
30. As an Admin, I want inviting a current member refused, so that I notice they are already here.
31. As an Admin, I want a second invitation to an address with one waiting to replace the first, so that only the newest link works.
32. As an Admin, I want an invitation to last seven days, so that one sent on a Friday survives the weekend.
33. As an Admin, I want to see waiting invitations with their role and expiry, so that I know who has not joined yet.
34. As an Admin, I want to resend a waiting invitation, so that a lost or failed email is not a dead end.
35. As an Admin, I want to cancel a waiting invitation, so that an address typed wrong never becomes a member.
36. As an Admin, I want each invitation, resend and cancellation on the audit log, so that the path into the workspace is on record.

### Accepting

37. As an invited person, I want the link to take me to an accept page that has me sign in with the invited address first, so that joining is one short path.
38. As an invited person with no display name yet, I want to be asked for one before I join, so that my colleagues see who I am.
39. As an invited person, I want accepting to make me a member with the invited role and take me into the workspace, so that I start at once.
40. As a person signed in with a different address, I want accepting refused with a word that says the invitation is for another address, so that an invitation cannot be taken by whoever holds the link.
41. As an invited person, I want an expired or cancelled invitation refused with a word that says so, so that I know to ask for a new one.
42. As an Admin, I want every acceptance on the audit log, and never a member without one, so that the audit log is a complete account of who joined.

### Access requests

43. As a signed-in person who belongs to no workspace, I want to ask to join a workspace by its slug with a reason, so that I have a way in that does not depend on an Admin thinking of me.
44. As a person asking to join, I want the same acknowledgement whatever I typed, so that the form cannot be used to find which companies are customers.
45. As an Admin, I want waiting requests listed with the requester's name, address and reason, so that I can decide each.
46. As an Admin, I want to approve a request with a role, which sends the same invitation email a direct invite sends, so that approved people join the same way as invited ones.
47. As an Admin, I want to decline a request, so that a stranger's ask is closed.

### Groups

48. As an Admin, I want a Groups view listing each group with its member count, so that I can see how the workspace is divided.
49. As an Admin, I want to create, rename and delete groups and add and remove members from them, so that audiences name the right people.
50. As an Admin, I want a member's groups shown on their row and when I open them, so that I see what they can read at a glance.

### The audit log

51. As an Admin, I want an Audit log view of every audit event in my workspace, newest first, so that I can answer *who did what, when*.
52. As an Admin, I want to filter the audit log by family — people, knowledge, sources, platform — so that I can look at one kind of act.
53. As an Admin, I want each event's actor named by display name, *a former member* when that person's name is gone, and *the platform* for the platform's own acts, so that I read people, not ids.
54. As an Admin, I want to see my workspace's audit log and nobody else's, and never the identity-set audit log, so that nothing about other companies reaches me.

### Display names

55. As an Admin, I want to flag an inappropriate display name, so that the operator corrects it.
56. As an Admin, I want every flag answered *sent to the operator*, whoever the person is, so that the answer tells me nothing about other workspaces.
57. As the operator, I want each flag emailed to me and waiting in the console, so that none is lost.

### The one door

58. As the platform's owner, I want every People act to go through the members slice, so that every one writes its audit event.
59. As the platform's owner, I want the organisation plugin's write endpoints and its slug check refused for every role, so that no raw request changes a membership unrecorded or tests whether a company is a customer.
60. As a member of one workspace, I want every People act refused against another workspace, so that isolation holds verb by verb.
61. As a person reading People, I want no screen to say *organisation* or *organization*, so that the platform speaks of workspaces throughout.

## Implementation Decisions

### The members slice

- **The slice gains the People acts**, each declared with its admission and its audit acts, each taking the Admin principal except where noted: list members; read one member; change a role; remove a member; revoke credentials in this workspace; invite; resend an invitation; cancel an invitation; list waiting invitations; flag a display name; read the audit log. The group acts and the access-request acts already there are wired as they stand.
- **Every act keys by (workspace, person id)**, never the member row's key, which names nothing of ours.
- **`requests.ts` converts to registered refusal words as its first step** (T-231's rule, amended 22/09/2026): its unions are built from the slice's registered words, and the register walk then reads the whole slice.
- **New refusal words are registered in the members vocabulary with a class by remedy** (ADR 0043): `last-admin` (*precondition* — promote someone else first); `already-a-member` (*conflict*); `no-such-invitation` (*absent*); `invitation-expired` (*precondition* — a new invitation comes first); `invitation-for-another-address` (*forbidden*); `no-such-role` registered as it already answers. `no-display-name`, registered by the workspaces slice, is reused for accept.
- **Change a role**: the new role is one of the three; the change writes `people.member.role_changed` with the old and new role; roles are re-read on every call, so it applies on the member's next request.
- **The last-Admin rule**: a role change or removal that would leave the workspace with no Admin refuses `last-admin`, judged inside the act's transaction under a lock over the workspace's Admin member rows, so two concurrent acts cannot both pass. The transport already holds the caller's own member row `FOR SHARE`, so two Admins removing each other at once can deadlock; the store maps Postgres's deadlock abort to a *conflict* answer, never a failure. Self-demotion and self-removal pass while another Admin remains. Revoking the last Admin's credentials is allowed. Erasure does not check (ADR 0020, amended 24/09/2026).
- **Remove a member** deletes the member row — group memberships cascade — revokes the tokens whose consented workspace is this one, and writes `people.member.removed` (actor the Admin, subject the person). The audit event is the record of the membership that was (ADR 0014, amended 24/09/2026). The person's sessions stay alive for their other workspaces and are refused here as *not a member*.
- **Revoke credentials in this workspace** writes the membership's revocation instant, revokes the tokens whose consented workspace is this one (the existing workspace-token revocation, re-used as a step), and writes `people.member.credentials_revoked` with the Admin as actor and the person as subject. The resolver already refuses a credential issued before the instant. Nothing outside the workspace changes and nothing tells the Admin whether other workspaces exist. *Revoke everywhere* is T-028's and unreachable from here.
- **One invitation step** mints an invitation for a direct invite and for an approved access request alike: the address stored lower-case, the role one of the three, expiry seven days on both paths (the identity set's expiry constant moves from 48 hours), one waiting invitation per (workspace, address) held by a partial unique index; a new invitation to an address with one waiting cancels the old one in the same transaction. It writes `people.invitation.created`; an approval writes its own `people.request.approved` beside it as today.
- **Invite** refuses `already-a-member` for a current member of this workspace and otherwise answers the same whether or not the address belongs to a person on the platform. **Resend** writes `people.invitation.resent`; **cancel** writes `people.invitation.cancelled`.
- **The invitation email is sent by the api after the act commits**, through the existing email transport, with the accept link. A send that fails leaves the invitation standing and answers that the email did not go, so the Admin resends.
- **Accept is the members slice's act at the auth boundary** — the access request's precedent (ADR 0038): the transport verifies a live session, and core runs under the platform principal with the person as actor. It refuses `no-such-invitation`, `invitation-expired`, `invitation-for-another-address` (the session person's verified address against the invitation's, case-insensitively), `already-a-member` and `no-display-name`. In one transaction it marks the invitation accepted, inserts the member row with the invitation's role, sets the session's active workspace and writes `people.member.joined` with the invitation id and role. Better Auth's accept endpoint and its after-hook are not used (ADR 0009, amended 24/09/2026).
- **Flag a display name** writes the workspace's `people.person.name_flagged` and an identity-set event naming the workspace (`people.name_flag.raised`), both with no name in the detail, and the api emails the operator after commit — the workspace, the person id and the current name — at the operator address the deployment configures (T-028's mark does not exist yet). It answers the same for every person; a second flag while one waits answers the same and writes nothing new. An Admin has no act that changes a display name.
- **Read the audit log** reads the workspace's own audit events through the tenant door, newest first, paged, with an optional family filter; actors resolve to display names from the person row, *a former member* when blank, and platform actors to *the platform*. It never reads the identity-set audit log.
- ***Checked by* reads the checker's name from their person row by person id**, not through the membership, so it stands after removal. An erased checker's wording (*a former member*) is its own ticket (T-355's out-of-scope line).

### The organisation plugin

- **Disabled paths** (ADR 0009, amended 24/09/2026): `invite-member`, `cancel-invitation`, `accept-invitation`, `reject-invitation`, `update-member-role`, `remove-member`, `leave`, `create`, `update`, `delete`, and the read `check-slug`. Kept: `set-active` (the picker's one write), `list`, `get-invitation` and `list-user-invitations`. `add-member` is server-only and never mounted. The two T-004 fences on invitation create and accept, their *not implemented* answer and their tests are deleted; the endpoints they fenced are now refused outright.
- **Consequences recorded, not built**: renaming a workspace becomes an operator ops command (T-028); no member leaves a workspace by themself in v0.1 — an Admin removes them, or an erasure ends the membership.

### Transport

- **A `members` tRPC router** carries every People act over the existing query and mutation base procedures, the Admin admission core's. **Accept** and **ask to join** ride a signed-in person's procedure, the one the display-name act uses, since the caller holds no membership.
- **Ask to join**: the refused screen offers a form taking a slug and a reason; its procedure calls the existing access-request act and always shows the neutral acknowledgement; its rate limit rides the auth counters (T-061's note).

### The SPA

- **People's views on the one list** become Members (default, `/people/members`), Groups, Owners, Thresholds, Erasure and suppression, Tokens, Audit log (ADR 0017, amended 24/09/2026). Owners, Thresholds, Erasure and suppression and Tokens stay unbuilt.
- **Members' tabs** (Members · Invitations · Requests) and acts reach the toolbar through the view-toolbar seam (T-313); a select-then-act command reads the view's state slot. A member opens as a sheet.
- **The accept page** is a route outside the shell, like the display-name screen: an unsigned-in visitor signs in first and returns to it; a person with no display name passes the display-name screen first; on success the person lands in the workspace.
- **Porting the external UI's blocks**: blocks 1 (Members), 2 (A member), 4 (Invitations) and 5 (Access requests) are ported; block 2's plural roles become the one role; the *teams* column and multi-select become *groups*; block 6 (Audit) becomes the Audit log view; block 3 (Roles and permissions) and block 7 (Security) are not ported. The blocks keep their Phosphor icons through the design system's icon set; `lucide-react` stays only inside the vendored registry primitives, recorded as a one-line exception; the external UI's stylesheet is not copied (design-system tokens supply); the forked primitives are not copied — the ones the ported blocks need and the tree lacks arrive through the shadcn registry with their notices entries, and the table library the blocks use enters the web workspace. **Spec-time gate**: one block compiles against the real tree before the rest are ported.

## Testing Decisions

- **A good test drives behaviour through the seam a caller uses and asserts what a caller can see**: the answer, the rows that landed or did not, the audit events by act, the session's fate. None asserts a query, a lock or a component's internals.
- **The api harness is the primary seam.** Through the tRPC procedures: the per-role, per-verb matrix — Admin allowed, Editor and Viewer refused — on list, role change, removal, revocation, invite, resend, cancel, request decisions, group acts, flag and audit log read (T-004's D2 cells, moved from the plugin's endpoints to the procedures); a member of one workspace refused on every verb against another (one test per verb); every disabled plugin endpoint refused for every role, one test each, `check-slug` among them; after a removal and after a revocation, **every** session the member holds — more than one, made by separate clients — refused in this workspace, and one of them still admitted in the person's other workspace; a revoked Admin's session refused on the People procedures (T-004's D4, moved); tokens consented to this workspace revoked and a token for another workspace untouched; the accept path end to end (invite, email captured, sign in as the invitee, accept, member with the role, `people.member.joined` present) and each of its refusals, with no member row and no event on a refusal; the invite answer identical for a known and an unknown address; the email-failed answer and the resend. Prior art: the OAuth-flow suite's organisation-endpoint and revocation cases, the update-user refusal test, the display-name procedure tests.
- **The members slice's own suite** for what only it can drive: two concurrent removals of the last two Admins leaving one; the deadlock answered *conflict*; two concurrent invitations to one address leaving one waiting; *Checked by* naming a removed checker. Prior art: the groups and access-request suites, the concepts suite's *Checked by* case.
- **The register walk** reads the whole members slice once `requests.ts` is converted; the new words appear in the register with their classes.
- **The browser suite** over every People surface: the Members view and its tabs, a member's sheet, the invite dialog, the accept page (sign-in, display name, accept), the Groups view, the Audit log view, the ask-to-join form; keyboard and screen-reader paths and the accessibility gate; and one test that no rendered People string contains *organisation* or *organization*. Prior art: the Sources screen's and the display-name screen's specs.
- **The failed-tRPC-procedure row** of the 9 September mutation findings (§2.4, placed with P1): a People mutation that fails partway, with the rows asserted absent; the membership read failing under a lock; and the membership procedure called over tRPC.
