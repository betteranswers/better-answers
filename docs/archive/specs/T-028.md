# T-028 — The operator: a third principal kind with a mark only an ops command sets, revoke everywhere, sign-ins on the identity-set audit log, and the console over every workspace

*Spec written 25 September 2026 from the owner's grill of 24/09/2026 over T-027 and T-028 (eighteen decisions over five rounds, reviewed the same day by an independent architecture pass), the amendments it wrote to ADRs 0009, 0017, 0020 and 0038, the glossary as that grill left it, T-028's lines and notes, T-063's and T-328's specs, T-355, the T-022 research on Better Auth's `admin()` plugin, and the route's P2 block. T-028's lines 3 and 4 — provisioning and first membership — landed as T-328; this spec is the rest of P2.*

## Problem Statement

The platform has an operator — the owner, working with Claude — and no way to be one. Every operator act today runs as the platform itself through an ops command, so nothing records which person acted. *Revoke everywhere*, the act that ends every session and token a person holds, exists in core and has no caller: if a client's person lost a laptop, nobody could end their access across workspaces except by hand in the database. Nobody can see who holds which workspaces, which sessions and clients a person has, or which workspaces exist, without a SQL prompt. Sign-ins are log lines, which nothing reads back as a record.

The People screen (T-027) sends Admins' display-name flags to the operator, who has nowhere to see them and no act to correct a name. And Better Auth's `admin()` plugin, which T-028 was first cut around, would add endpoints that write no audit event and a session revoke that leaves OAuth tokens alive.

## Solution

The operator becomes a person the platform knows: a mark on their person row, set and cleared only by an ops command, cleared by erasure. When that person signs in with their ordinary email code, the console's procedures act as an **operator principal** — a third kind beside the user and the platform principal — and never from an OAuth token. `admin()` is not installed.

The work comes in two parts:

- **The security floor** — the operator principal kind and its mark; *revoke everywhere* re-typed to the operator and recorded on the identity-set audit log; each sign-in and every operator write recorded there, and each consent recorded in the consented workspace's audit log; every console act proven refused to anyone who is not the operator, to a token, and — for the writes — to a sign-in older than an hour. T-331, the first client's production run, waits on this part. The console's reads, the name correction and the workspace rename command land beside it.
- **The console** — a surface of its own at `/console`, outside any workspace, on the same shell regions: **People** (every person, their workspaces and role in each, their last sign-in; one person's sessions and client grants; *revoke everywhere* and *correct display name*; the names waiting for correction) and **Workspaces** (each with its member count, read-only). T-331 does not wait on it.

## User Stories

### Becoming the operator

1. As the owner, I want to make a person the operator by one ops command naming their address, so that the platform knows who its operator is without a database edit.
2. As the owner, I want the same command to clear the mark, so that an operator who leaves loses the role.
3. As the owner, I want the command to refuse a person who has not signed in, so that the mark is never set on nobody.
4. As the owner, I want setting and clearing the mark on the identity-set audit log under the platform's own actor id, so that the role's history is on record.
5. As a person carrying the mark, I want nothing to change for me inside a workspace — I am a member with the role I hold there — so that operator power never leaks into a workspace's own screens.
6. As a person whose erasure request is carried out, I want the operator mark cleared with the rest of my identity, so that erasure leaves no role behind.
7. As the platform's owner, I want no screen able to set the mark, so that becoming the operator needs access to the platform's own tooling.

### Acting as the operator

8. As the operator, I want to reach the console from my ordinary signed-in session, so that I need no second credential.
9. As the operator, I want a link to the console in the top bar's menu, shown to me alone, so that others never see a door they cannot use.
10. As a person who is not the operator, I want every console act refused with a word that says only the operator may, so that the refusal is clear and tells me nothing else.
11. As the platform's owner, I want every console act refused to an OAuth token, so that a Claude connection can never reach operator acts.
12. As the operator, I want *revoke everywhere* and *correct display name* to ask me to sign in again when my sign-in is more than an hour old, so that a stolen session cookie cannot use them.
13. As the operator, I want my reads to be quick and unrecorded, so that looking is cheap and the identity-set audit log holds only what changed.

### People

14. As the operator, I want to list every person on the platform with their display name, address, the workspaces they belong to and their role in each, and their last sign-in, so that I can answer who is where.
15. As the operator, I want to search people by name or address, so that I can find one person fast.
16. As the operator, I want *last sign-in* read from the identity-set audit log rather than live sessions, so that it survives a revocation, an expiry or an erasure of the session rows.
17. As the operator, I want to open a person and see their sessions — when each began and was last used — and their client grants — which client, which workspace it was consented to, when it was issued and last used, whether revoked — so that I can see what could act as them.

### Revoke everywhere

18. As the operator, I want to revoke a person's credentials everywhere, so that a compromised person's every session and token ends in every workspace at once.
19. As the operator, I want revoke everywhere to leave the person free to sign in again, so that it ends what was issued and does not bar them.
20. As the operator, I want each revoke everywhere on the identity-set audit log with me as actor and the person as subject, so that the incident response is on record.
21. As an Admin, I want revoke everywhere to reach my workspace too, so that one act covers every company the person is in.

### Display names

22. As the operator, I want a list of names waiting for correction — each person flagged, which workspaces flagged them and when — so that no flag is lost if an email is missed.
23. As the operator, I want each flag emailed to me, so that I know to look.
24. As the operator, I want to correct a person's display name under the same rule a person's own name follows, so that a correction is never something a person could not have typed.
25. As the operator, I want a correction to take a person off the waiting list, so that the list shows only what still waits.
26. As the operator, I want each correction on the identity-set audit log with no name in it, so that the name has one copy, which erasure can blank.

### Workspaces

27. As the operator, I want to list every workspace with its name, slug, member count and creation date, so that I see the estate at a glance.
28. As the operator, I want to rename a workspace by an ops command, so that a company that renames itself is renamed here without an Admin screen.
29. As the operator, I want renaming on that workspace's audit log, so that its Admins can see it happened.
30. As the operator, I want provisioning and adding a member to stay the ops commands they are, so that the proven path is unchanged.

### The record

31. As the operator, I want each sign-in on the identity-set audit log — who and when, never an address, an IP or a user agent — so that sign-ins are records, not log lines, and hold no personal data erasure would have to rewrite.
32. As an Admin, I want each consent — a person connecting a client such as Claude to my workspace — on my workspace's audit log, so that I see who connected what.
33. As whoever runs the platform, I want a token's issue, refusal and refresh and a workspace pick to stay log lines, so that the audit logs hold decisions and not hourly noise.
34. As the platform's owner, I want a sign-in or consent to succeed even if writing its record fails, with the failure a log line, so that the record never locks a person out.

### The console's shape

35. As the operator, I want the console to use the same rail, secondary nav and top bar as Control Centre, so that it feels like the same product.
36. As the operator, I want the top bar to read *Console* with no role and a way back to my workspaces, so that I always know I am outside any one company.
37. As the operator, I want the console's two screens, People and Workspaces, on its own list and never among Control Centre's six, so that no workspace screen gains a cross-workspace view.

## Implementation Decisions

### The operator principal

- **A third principal kind**, beside the user and platform principals (ADR 0009, amendment of 2026-09-04 and of 2026-09-24): it carries the person id and the credential's issue instant, and no workspace, role or groups, so it can never be passed where a tenant function expects a user principal. Admission gains the operator kind; console acts admit it alone.
- **The mark** is a column on the identity set's person row that the platform owns — never input from a sign-in or update body, never returned by the library. It is set and cleared by one ops command at the `runOps` seam, which refuses a person who has not signed in and writes `people.operator.granted` or `people.operator.revoked` to the identity-set audit log under the platform's own actor id (the ops runner cannot prove which person runs it; ADR 0009, 2026-09-22). Erasure clears it with the rest of the identity step (ADR 0020, amended 24/09/2026).
- **Resolution**: the tRPC context builds an operator principal only from a signed-in session whose person carries the mark and passes the person-level revocation check; a bearer token never yields one. Anything else asking for a console act refuses `not-the-operator` (*forbidden*).
- **Freshness**: revoke everywhere and correct display name refuse `sign-in-too-old` (*unauthenticated* — sign in again) when the session row's own creation instant is more than an hour old; the library's refresh moves only its update and expiry instants, and its own freshness setting governs only its endpoints.

### The acts

- **List people** reads the identity set alone: person id, display name, address, each membership's workspace and role, the last `people.person.signed_in` instant, the person-level revocation instant. This is the one cross-workspace read kept (ADR 0009, amended 24/09/2026). Paged and searchable.
- **List workspaces**: id, name, slug, member count, created.
- **Inspect a person**: their sessions (created, last used, expiry) and their OAuth grants (client, consented workspace, issued, last used, revoked).
- **Revoke everywhere** is the existing person-level revocation re-typed from the platform principal to the operator principal: the person's revocation instant, their sessions ended, their refresh and access tokens revoked, and `people.person.credentials_revoked` written to the identity-set audit log with the operator as actor and the person as subject, in the same transaction.
- **Correct a display name** runs T-355's rule and write under the operator principal, writing `people.person.renamed` with no name in the detail.
- **Names waiting**: every person with a `people.name_flag.raised` event (T-027) later than their last correction, with the flagging workspaces and instants.
- **Rename a workspace** is an ops command: name and slug, the slug refusing `slug-taken`, writing `platform.workspace.renamed` to that workspace's audit log. Before the build, the spec-time check confirms nothing but the access request and the picker addresses a workspace by its slug.
- **Reads write nothing** (ADR 0043).
- **The flag email** (T-027) goes to every person carrying the mark once this lands, in place of the configured operator address.

### The identity-set audit log

- **Sign-in** writes `people.person.signed_in` — actor and subject the person, empty detail — from the library's after-hook on a successful email-code sign-in. **Consent** writes `people.client.consented` with the client id to the consented workspace's audit log from the OAuth provider's consent hook. Both are written after the library's own write and outside any transaction: the one place the record and the act do not land or fail together (ADR 0009, ADR 0038, amended 24/09/2026). The hooks never throw; a failed write is a log line.
- A token issued, refused or refreshed and a workspace pick stay log lines.

### Transport

- **A `console` tRPC router** on its own base procedure that resolves the operator principal, with the freshness check on the two writes.
- **`admin()` is not installed**; there is no admin-plugin migration and no admin-plugin disabled path.

### The console

- **`/console`** is a route outside any workspace. The shell's rail, secondary nav and top bar take their screens from a list instead of reading Control Centre's one list directly, and the console passes its own: **People** (views: *Everyone*, *Names waiting*) and **Workspaces**. The top bar reads *Console*, shows no role and links back to the workspace picker. A person without the mark who opens `/console` sees the refused state.
- **People**: a searchable list; a person opens as a sheet with sessions and grants and the two acts behind a confirmation; the freshness refusal sends the operator to sign in and back. **Workspaces**: a read-only list.
- Built from the platform's own components under the design skill; block 7's session list is reference for the sheet.

### The split

- **Part A, the security floor** — the principal kind, the mark and its command, erasure clearing it, the console router's refusals (T-409); revoke everywhere with the one-hour rule (T-410); sign-in and consent records (T-411). T-331 waits on part A alone (owner, 25/09/2026).
- **Beside it, not gating T-331** — the console's reads (T-412), the name correction and names waiting (T-413, after T-027's flag act, T-408), the workspace rename command (T-414).
- **Part B, the console screens** — the shell's parametrisation and the Workspaces screen (T-415), the People screen and revoke everywhere (T-416), names waiting (T-417).

## Testing Decisions

- **A good test drives behaviour through the seam a caller uses and asserts what a caller can see**: the answer, the rows that landed, the sessions and tokens that died, the audit events by act.
- **The api harness is the primary seam**: every console procedure refused `not-the-operator` to a workspace Admin, an Editor and a Viewer, and refused to an OAuth bearer; the two writes refused `sign-in-too-old` on a session older than an hour and admitted on a fresh one; revoke everywhere across a person in two workspaces ending every session and every token in both, the identity-set event present, and a fresh sign-in admitted afterwards; list people naming each workspace and role; last sign-in read from the sign-in events and surviving the session's deletion; a sign-in writing `people.person.signed_in` with no address, IP or user agent anywhere in the row; a consent writing `people.client.consented` to the consented workspace; a sign-in still succeeding when its record's write is made to fail; the names-waiting list emptied by a correction. Prior art: the OAuth-flow suite's revocation cases, the display-name procedure tests.
- **The ops command tests**: the mark set and cleared, refusing an unknown person, each writing its identity-set event under the platform's actor id, **which a test reads back** (§2.4's platform-actor-id row of the 9 September mutation findings, placed with P2); the workspace rename and its `slug-taken` refusal. Prior art: the `provision-workspace` and `add-member` cases.
- **Core's erasure suite**: erasure clears the operator mark.
- **The browser suite**: the console reachable from the top bar for the operator and absent for everyone else; `/console` refused to a non-operator; People and Workspaces render; a person's sheet shows sessions and grants; revoke everywhere and correct display name through their confirmations; the freshness round-trip; keyboard, screen-reader and accessibility gate. Prior art: the shell's and the Sources screen's specs.

## Out of Scope

- Better Auth's `admin()` plugin in any form: impersonation, banning, a single-session revoke, deleting a person (erasure is the only way out of the identity set), setting a password, creating a user.
- Provisioning or adding a member from a screen; setting the operator mark from a screen.
- The operator reading any workspace's audit log; recording the operator's reads.
- A second factor or step-up beyond the one-hour sign-in rule.
- The Admin's People acts, the flag act and the audit log view — T-027.
- Renaming the code's *ledger* identifiers — the hygiene change after the work in flight on 24/09/2026 merges.
