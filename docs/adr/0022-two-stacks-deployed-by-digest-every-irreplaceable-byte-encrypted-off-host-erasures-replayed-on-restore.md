# Two stacks and a database resource, deployed by digest; every irreplaceable byte encrypted and copied off-host under governance lock; erasures replayed on restore; the restore drilled monthly and staging wiped after

The platform runs on two IONOS VPS private networks (ADR 0005, ticket 38): **VPC 1** holds production; **VPC 2** holds Coolify (pinned, auto-update off), staging and the restore target. On VPC 1, **Postgres is a Coolify database resource** and everything else is **two Docker Compose stacks** — `stores` (the object store, Neo4j, Forgejo, the CPU embedding host, `cloudflared`, `backup`, `init`), redeployed only for a store's upgrade, and `platform` (`migrate` → `app` → `worker`), redeployed on every release — because a Coolify redeploy stops and restarts the whole resource: one stack would restart the tunnel and every store on every release. `depends_on` conditions are a convenience inside a stack; the safety is the runtime guard (`migrate` waits for the DSN; the worker refuses to claim on a schema mismatch — ADR 0007). Every stateful service is a **bind mount under `/data/<service>`**, owned by its uid by an idempotent `init` step, never root.

**Code lives in a private GitHub repository under its own organisation** (private until ticket 62 decides; the flip changes nothing laid down); `check` runs on hosted Ubuntu runners per PR; mutation testing runs **weekly** there (nightly again on a self-hosted runner). One image per tier is built on `main`, pushed to `ghcr.io` and **deployed by digest** — `${APP_IMAGE_DIGEST:?}` refuses to start without one; staging redeploys on every build; production is promoted by a **release a person runs** (PATCH the digest variables, then Coolify's deploy call), recorded as an `audit_event` (*release*). Migrations are forward-only; a rollback is the previous digest. Renovate pins digests, ignores `lifts/**`, never automerges; the Neo4j and Forgejo LTS lines are frozen — a new line is an upgrade drill.

**The object store is Garage** (AGPL-3.0, run unmodified over S3 like Neo4j's GPL over Bolt; one binary, single-node; no versioning or lock in the primary store — immutability lives off-host). MinIO is end-of-life (server archived 25/04/2026, `mc` 14/07/2026) and never named again.

**Backups** (`deploy/BACKUPS.md`): the `backup` service dumps Postgres **hourly (48 h) · daily (30 d) · weekly (8 w) · monthly (6 m)**, **client-side encrypted with `age`**, verified after upload, to an off-host **dumps bucket** (Backblaze B2, EU Central — UK preferred, EU fine) with versioning and **object lock in governance mode**: the host's credential can write and list, never delete or bypass; only the escrowed admin credential can. The object store is mirrored nightly with `rclone sync` to a second, versioned, **unlocked** bucket so erasure deletions propagate; every Forgejo repository is bundled nightly (verified, encrypted) and push-mirrored to VPC 2 over SSH; Coolify's instance backup goes to the dumps bucket, its SSH keys to escrow. No WAL archiving until the database passes 5 GB or a contract states an RPO under an hour. Neo4j, every per-binding LMDB, the working trees and caches are never backed up: the first is rebuilt, the second is personal data disposable by design (capped at 4 GB per binding).

**Every dump is a personal-data copy** — it holds `suppression`, `erasure_request`, findings and pre-rewrite history. So (ADR 0020 amended) the erasure report's dates are computed from the **last dump before the rewrite** (48 h · 30 d · 8 w · 6 m), the hourly dump waits while the routine holds `pg_advisory_lock(41)`, and **every restore replays the erasures completed after the dump** before `app` turns healthy. A restore is an `audit_event` (*restore*).

**The drill** (`deploy/restore-drill.sh`) runs monthly from host cron on VPC 2: staging wiped, restored from the buckets with erasures replayed, one workspace's graph rebuilt and its counts diffed per label (ADR 0021), the sweeper exercised, `find` · a guide read · `ask` smoke-tested, the bucket listing checked against the matrix, **RTO and RPO recorded** on a `backup_run(kind = drill)` row — and **staging wiped and re-seeded with the synthetic fixture at the end**; every third drill rehearses the erasure routine on a synthetic subject and greps the restored dump for its tokens. Staging holds synthetic data outside a drill; ticket 61's prototype runs against it.

**Secrets** (`deploy/SECRETS.md`): bootstrap secrets in Coolify's per-resource env, never in git; six items escrowed in a password-manager vault with **two holders** before the first client goes live (Coolify `APP_KEY` and SSH keys, the KEK, the backup `age` identity, the bucket admin credential, the tunnel token) plus the `ghcr.io` pull token, the mirror deploy key and every account's recovery codes; the table shows the bus factor.

**The edge**: one Cloudflare tunnel, four hostnames — `app.` behind Access (email OTP), `mcp.` open (`/mcp`, discovery and `/oauth/*` — OAuth pages cannot sit behind Access), **`agent.` open and routed only to `/agent/v1/*`**, refused before any body is read, for the share agent (ADR 0008 amended), the apex answering 404 on `/c/`; the catch-all `http_status:404`; `docs.` on Cloudflare Pages. **The share agent's per-file cap is 100 MB** — Cloudflare's edge body limit on the Free and Pro plans, enforced before the origin. Cloudflare Free with its one rate-limiting rule on `agent.`; `mcp.` is limited per token in the app.

**Watching**: a hosted dead-man's switch (healthchecks.io) — the scheduler pings every minute; every backup job pings **only after its upload is verified**, with a body of the outcome word and sizes and nothing else; checks for the drill, *staging-wiped* and Coolify's backup; Docker health checks on every service and Coolify's notifications to email; nothing inbound.

## Alternatives considered

- **One compose stack for everything** — one file, but an outage per release once Coolify's redeploy behaviour is known. Rejected.
- **Every store its own Coolify resource** — each with a UI, no `depends_on`; more resources to keep in step. Rejected for v0.1.
- **Compliance-mode lock for twelve months** — turns "we choose not to delete" into "we cannot", which a regulator reads as failure to design for erasure; unfixable on a scripting mistake. Governance mode with the bypass right only in escrow gives the same ransomware guarantee. Returns only on a lawyer's say-so.
- **pgBackRest WAL archiving from day one** — minutes instead of an hour of recovery point, for a second process with its own failure modes on a founder-run box; the triggers are stated.
- **Forgejo Actions or a self-hosted runner from day one** — a second CI to run on the restore target; the `runs-on` change is the growth step.
- **SeaweedFS** as the object store — Apache-2.0 and the full S3 feature list, but several daemons even in one process; operational simplicity won for one VPS. Pinning MinIO's last image (an unpatched CVE) was rejected outright.
- **Access service tokens** for the share agent — a second, Cloudflare-shaped credential class; the hostname is one ingress line and one host check. Kept as the day-two kill switch.
- **A self-hosted dead-man's switch on VPC 2** — cannot report its own outage.
- **SOPS in the repository** for secrets — auditable, one more tool, and Coolify still needs the values pasted.

## Amendment — 2026-08-28, no Neo4j in the stores stack; a third image by digest (ticket 73, ADR 0023)

The `stores` stack is the object store, Forgejo, the CPU embedding host, `cloudflared`, `backup` and `init` — Neo4j is gone, with `/data/neo4j` and `NEO4J_AUTH`. The Postgres resource runs the platform's own image (`deploy/postgres.Dockerfile`: AGE + pgvector), the third image `build.yml` pushes to `ghcr.io` and Renovate pins by digest; the AGE line is frozen like Forgejo's — a new Postgres major is an upgrade drill. "Neo4j … never backed up" is struck: the graph rides every Postgres dump and every restore; the drill still rebuilds one workspace's graph and diffs its counts, now as a repair-path rehearsal. Everything else stands.

## Amendment — 2026-08-28, two 4 GB boxes, staging on demand, the forge is bare git (ticket 74, ADR 0024)

The estate is two boxes of 4 vCPU · 4 GB · 120 GB, not 16 + 8 GB: VPC 1 runs all of production with the worker capped at 1.5 GB and one index at a time, `shared_buffers` 512 MB and a 4 GB swap file; VPC 2 runs Coolify, the git mirror and a restore target that exists only during a drill — **staging is on demand**, so "staging redeploys on every build" reads "the image is pushed; staging deploys when brought up". No embedding host runs in the first estate (client one is on the hosted route, ADR 0020). **Forgejo is replaced** by bare git repositories under `/data/git` that the app writes; the nightly bundles and the push mirror to VPC 2 are the `backup` service's, over SSH as before; "Forgejo LTS line frozen" and "Forgejo's schema" in the dump are struck. The growth steps are named in ADR 0024 (a WireGuard split, then a third contract). Everything else stands, including the first drill before the first client's data.

**Amended by ADR 0025 (28/08/2026):** "Docker health checks and Coolify's notifications" gains the app's own alert email through `SMTP_URL` and the rule that a signal is a query over rows; Coolify's notification channel is Resend.

## Amendment — 2026-09-02, `app.` is open at the edge (T-022 grilling, ADR 0009 amended)

"`app.` behind Access (email OTP)" is struck. Better Auth is the product's sign-in (ADR 0009), the SPA signs in on `app.` and shares one session with `mcp.`, and Cloudflare Access in front of it was a second email-OTP the person had to pass to reach the first. `app.` joins `mcp.` as an open hostname behind the tunnel; the app's own per-IP counters and Better Auth's limiter are the abuse controls, as they already are for `mcp.`. Access stays available as the day-two kill switch it already is for `agent.`. Everything else stands.

## Amendment — 2026-09-03, `/oauth2/*` and the second fence (T-030, PR #10)

Two corrections of wording, no change of decision. The edge section's "`/oauth/*`" reads **`/oauth2/*`**: that is the path `@better-auth/oauth-provider` mounts and the one T-004 shipped and tested; the shorthand predates the library choice. And the sentence "refused before any body is read" is now enacted in the app as well as at the edge: `apps/api/src/ingress/hostnames.ts` holds one list of surface → hostnames → reason and refuses, before any counter, session read or body, a path outside its hostname's surface. The tunnel's ingress rules remain the first fence; the app's list is the second, because Better Auth's handler answers the wildcard on every hostname the process is given. The four hostnames are bootstrap env the app refuses to start without.

## Amendment — 2026-09-03 (later), one hostname source (T-039)

A correction of wording, no change of decision. "The four hostnames are bootstrap env the app refuses to start without" reads: **three** are bootstrap env — `APP_HOSTNAME`, `AGENT_HOSTNAME`, `APEX_HOSTNAME` — and the fourth, `mcp.`, is `PUBLIC_URL`'s host, which the app derives rather than being told twice. `PUBLIC_URL` is bootstrap env the app already refuses to start without, so all four hostnames still come from the deploy unit and a missing or malformed one still stops the process; all four must still differ, the derived one included. The estate still has four hostnames and the fence still holds all four.

## Amendment — 2026-09-03 (T-045), three hostnames, and `docs.` struck

The edge section's "four hostnames" reads **three**: `app.` carries the product, `/mcp`, discovery, `/jwks` and `/oauth2/*` on one origin, `agent.` is routed only to `/agent/v1/*`, and the apex answers 404 on `/c/`. **`mcp.` is gone** — its surfaces are paths on `app.` and its issuer moved with them (ADR 0034), which supersedes the amendment above: three hostnames come from the deploy unit — `app.` derived from `PUBLIC_URL`, with no `APP_HOSTNAME` variable; `AGENT_HOSTNAME` and `APEX_HOSTNAME` given as bootstrap env — all of which must differ or the app refuses to start. "`docs.` on Cloudflare Pages" is **struck**: there is no public documentation site at this stage, and the ops documents' public halves live unrendered in the docs-site source (T-044). The tunnel therefore has three ingress rules, and Cloudflare's rate-limit rules match **paths on `app.`** rather than hostnames. Everything else stands.
