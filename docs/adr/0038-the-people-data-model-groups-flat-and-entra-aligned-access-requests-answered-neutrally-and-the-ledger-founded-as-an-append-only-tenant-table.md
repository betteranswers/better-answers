# The people data model: groups flat and Entra-aligned as the one grouping shape, access requests answered neutrally, and the ledger founded as a tenant table the database keeps append-only

**Where this came from.** The platform could say *how confidential* a piece of knowledge is but not *who it is for*: the glossary's *audience* names groups, and no group existed anywhere; the only door into a workspace was an Admin thinking to invite you; and the glossary and the constitution promised that every Admin act lands in *the one append-only ledger* while no `audit_event` table existed — the auth module wrote log lines, and a log line is not a ledger. This record is the T-048 spec (`apps/docs-site/specs/T-048.md`, 5 September 2026: the grill over `.scratch/readiness-2026-09-04/ui-wiring-and-t006.md`, the owner's decisions cut from it, and a coordination round with the concurrent T-006 spec), read against the identity decisions of ADR 0035 and the `[AUDIT]` rules T-079 wrote ahead of it. It lands with T-059, the first of three slices — the ledger, then groups (T-060), then access requests (T-061) — so that T-060 and T-061 code against a decision rather than making one.

**Groups are flat and Entra-aligned, and there is one grouping shape.** A *group* is a named set of members of one workspace — a platform-minted ULID id, a name unique per workspace, no parent reference of any kind — and a person may sit in several. Membership changes only what a person may *see*, through audiences, never what they may *do*, which is their role: `group_member` carries no role and no permission column, and a member removed from the workspace cascades out of every group. Every visibility check pays one membership lookup, which is the Entra access model taken as it stands; sub-groups, should they ever come, are an additive nesting migration and never a reversal. A group carries an **origin discriminator** — Admin-curated or audience-minted — a wide text column narrowed at the boundary to the closed pair exactly as sensitivity is, so that the implicit group a Restricted binding's named-people audience will one day mint is a row of this same table and no second grouping shape ever exists. Nothing mints one yet: neither T-060 nor T-006 does, and the discriminator is there so that the surface that does has nowhere else to put it. This is the reconciliation with T-006's audience decisions of 1 September 2026, agreed with its spec session: `audience_groups` holds exactly these group ids, member-lists-become-groups is the discriminator's implicit kind, and the empty-intersection-forces-Restricted rule and the cascade are the ground of T-006's own ADR, which cites this one for the ids and is cited here and not restated. **Deleting a group is allowed and fail-closed**: audience arrays carry group ids with no foreign key, so a deleted group leaves dangling ids, and the read predicate's fail-closed shape resolves them to no caller — the content narrows, never widens. A warning before the delete is a screen's concern.

**An access request is a signed-in non-member's recorded ask, answered neutrally.** The requester is a person id — someone who has signed in and holds no membership in the workspace they name — with a required reason; the status is one of waiting, approved, declined; one open request per workspace and requester is a partial unique index over the waiting status. There is no asks column, because a v0.1 request asks for exactly one thing, and no duration column, because temporal access is not adopted. The request act is **the auth-boundary case**: a Principal requires a membership and the requester's whole point is not having one, so the transport verifies the live session and core performs the write under the platform principal with the person as the ledger's actor, through the audit slice's second door — the same precedent every platform act already sets, and not a hole in the rule that every core function takes a Principal, because the platform principal is one. The act resolves the workspace by its slug and answers **one neutral acknowledgement** whether the slug resolves, does not resolve, names a workspace the person already belongs to, or names one they are already waiting on — writing a row only in the first case — so the surface can never enumerate workspaces or memberships; anti-enumeration is a functional test, and rate-limiting rides T-004's counters when the endpoint lands. **Approve** validates the chosen role against the three, mints the invitation row directly through the identity-write seam in the same transaction — a direct row write, no Better Auth import in core, never Better Auth's endpoint path — and records the invitation's id on the request; T-004's two invitation fences stand untouched until T-027 brings the accept page, because they fence endpoints this act never calls. **Decline** records who said no.

**The ledger is founded as an ordinary tenant table, append-only by the database.** `audit_event` is created through the RLS helper like every tenant table (ADR 0032), and that settles what it can hold: a row always belongs to a workspace, so an act with no workspace — a sign-in, a token issued or refused, the person-level *revoke credentials* that runs over the identity set — is a log line until an identity-set ledger exists (T-028), while **provisioning is a platform act** whose row lands in the workspace it creates, because the transaction already runs as that workspace (owner, 05/09/2026). The actor is the kernel's actor id in the form ADR 0035 fixed, and nothing here restates it; the detail carries ids and role words, each field declared with its kind so that no field can hold a name or a contact, and an erasure rewrites files and never the ledger. The id is caller-minted with no database default, so a governed write mints it before its git commit and the commit carries it (ADR 0014 rule 4). The act is `family.subject.verb` over the four families, and the database derives the family and the subject's kind from it as generated columns and refuses an act outside the four with a CHECK, so a screen filters by prefix and no second value can disagree with the act. A nullable batch id honours ADR 0014's rule 4 — a bulk act is N rows sharing one id, never one row hiding N. **Append-only is the database's guarantee**: the migration revokes `UPDATE` and `DELETE` from the app's role and everything from the worker's, and each refusal is tested beside the served path. **ADR 0014's month partitioning is superseded in words**: the table is not partitioned, because partitioning a policy-bearing table changes the catalogue assertion the RLS suite proves and a retention delete would need a role that is not the app's; a row-count trigger, not a date, is what reopens the question. The audit slice has two doors and no third, as ADR 0035 stated: `record` derives the actor from the caller's Principal, and `recordFor` takes the platform principal and an explicit actor, typed so a user principal cannot reach it, with the access request its one caller. Both run inside the caller's transaction and **reject on any failure** rather than returning a value — the one deliberate exception to the kernel's result convention, recorded there as its rule 5 — because a value the act might not read would let an act commit without its row, and the point of the ledger is that the two land or fail together.

**Not adopted.** *Teams* are not a platform word: a group may represent a team, and that is its name. *Plural roles* and *dynamic roles* are not adopted: a person holds one role in a workspace, and the three levels — Admin, Editor, Viewer — are ADR 0019's (its 2026-08-27 amendment) and stand. *Temporal access* — a duration on a request, a membership that expires — is Entra-PIM territory and is not adopted; nor is request expiry and its sweeper, nor a *Suspended* member state (ADR 0035). The external UI that supplies the People screens carries fields for several of these; the port trims to this model, not the other way round.

We decided this because a group that could nest would cost every visibility check a walk instead of a lookup, and would have to be reversed before Entra's model could be matched; because two grouping shapes — one for Admin-curated groups and one for a binding's named people — would be two tables an audience could name and two cascades to keep honest; because a request surface that answered differently for a real workspace and an unknown one would be an oracle over the tenant list, which is exactly what a signed-in stranger must not have; because a ledger that lived outside the tenant guarantee would be the one table a workspace's rows could leak through, and one that lived inside it cannot hold an act with no workspace, which is a limit worth stating rather than fudging; because append-only enforced in code is a convention the next migration forgets, while a revoked privilege is refused by the database to every caller including the ones not yet written; and because every non-adoption above is a concept the next session porting the external UI would otherwise reintroduce by accident.

## Considered options

- **Nested groups, or teams as a concept beside groups.** Rejected: a walk per visibility check, a second concept for the same set of people, and a reversal the day the Entra model is matched. Nesting stays an additive migration nobody has asked for.
- **A second table for a binding's named people.** Rejected: two grouping shapes an audience could name; the origin discriminator on one table is the whole difference.
- **A group with a role or a permission column.** Rejected: a back door to a role, and a second thing the resolver would read; a role is a level on the membership and nothing else.
- **Refuse the delete of a group an audience still names.** Rejected for the data model: the predicate is fail-closed, so a dangling id narrows content and never widens it; warning before the delete is the Groups screen's.
- **The request act under a user principal with a synthetic membership, or under no principal at all.** Rejected: the first invents a membership the person does not hold, the second a function in core with no Principal; the platform principal with the person named as actor is the precedent every platform act already sets.
- **Different answers for an unknown slug and an already-member.** Rejected: an enumeration oracle, and the whole reason the acknowledgement is neutral.
- **Approve through Better Auth's invitation endpoint.** Rejected: it would import the identity provider into core and cross the two fences T-004 keeps until the accept page exists; a direct row write in the same transaction is what the credentials-revocation act already does.
- **The ledger over the identity set from the start**, so a sign-in could be a row. Rejected: a table with no workspace column and no policy, which every tenant's acts would then sit in; the identity-set ledger is T-028's, and until then a workspace-less event is a log line.
- **Month partitioning, as ADR 0014 said.** Rejected: a partitioned policy-bearing table changes what the RLS suite proves per table, and a retention delete needs a role the app's is not; a row-count trigger reopens it.
- **Doors that return a `Result`.** Rejected: a value the calling act might not read would let its rows commit without their event; a rejection inside the transaction is what makes fail-together a guarantee rather than a discipline.
- **Plural roles, dynamic roles, temporal access, request expiry, a Suspended state.** Rejected as above; each would be a new concept in the glossary before it could be a column, and none has a v0.1 story.

## Consequences

- **Amends ADR 0014** by a dated amendment with its index row in the same commit: the ledger's month partitioning is superseded in words, the schema-spike consequence that depended on it is struck, and `team` leaves the identity-set list it named in passing.
- **`CONTEXT.md`**: *access request* (T-079) and *group* (05/09/2026) stand as written; *ledger* and *audit event* already carry the families and the actor form. No word changes here.
- **The code lands in three slices**: `audit_event`, the audit slice's vocabulary and two doors, provisioning's row and this record (T-059); `group`, `group_member` and the members slice's group acts (T-060); `access_request`, the request at the boundary, approve and decline (T-061). T-027 places the screens and wires the People acts; T-006's ADR cites this one for the group ids and owns the audience representation; T-028 founds the identity-set ledger and re-types *revoke everywhere*; T-052's governed write mints its ledger id before its commit.
- The kernel's result convention gains its rule 5, the transaction-internal rejection, with the audit doors as its case.
- The table-ownership map names `audit` as `audit_event`'s owner; the `[AUDIT]` family of `CODING_RULES.md` is the checkable form of the ledger paragraph above and is not restated here.
- Reopens nothing in ADRs 0001–0013, 0015–0034, 0036–0037; amends 0014; codes ADR 0035's stated interface.
