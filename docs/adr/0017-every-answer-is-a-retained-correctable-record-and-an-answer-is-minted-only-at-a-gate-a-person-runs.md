# Every answer is a retained, correctable record fixed at its level; an `Answer` is minted only at a gate a person runs; Control Centre is six screens

Every answer the platform gives — to a typed question, over MCP under the per-user principal, or as one response of a question set — is one row in the **answer audit**: a *skeleton* (surface, role, the predicate as columns, every citation as a structured row — concept IRI, content hash, cited span, marker label — with its trust at the time, `reused_from` and the judge's verdict, feedback verdicts, correction links, a maintained flag state) plus a *content* child partitioned by month (the question, the answer, who asked, notes). Content is kept **twelve months** by default — a workspace sets between 30 days and three years — and is then **thinned by dropping the partition**, held rows (an open correction) copied forward first; the skeleton is kept for good, so "cited in N answers" (distinct person and day, over answers the counting caller could have received) and the answer tests survive the text. A person's rows leave on an erasure request; the **records export** takes the skeleton and the text still under retention. A reader's **feedback** is *helpful*, or a **flag** with one of four reasons — *wrong* · *out of date* · *incomplete* · *should not have shown* — and the reason picks the level the fix happens at: *wrong* and *out of date* become a verification request (or an *edit* suggestion when the fix is typed) on a citation picked from the answer's own; *incomplete*, *should not have shown* and a flag on an answer that cited nothing go to an Admin, who picks the level. A **correction** is an Admin's or owner's act on one row that records its level — *concept* (the request, suggestion or conflict resolution it made), *source* (the binding act or citation repair) or *retrieval* — and links the act; a retrieval correction is kept as an **answer test**: its own copy of the question, a role never a person, the concepts the answer must reach and must not, the `Answer` it must or must not reuse, the expected verdict. The workspace's tests are **replayed retrieval-only** — entry points, walk and judge, never the draft — on a route, prompt or index change and weekly; a test whose concept is deprecated or discarded is *stale*, set in the commit; a test fails only on two consecutive runs; passed · failed · stale are three numbers. An **`Answer` concept is minted or updated only at the promotion gate**: a Bid writer *saves an answer* (from a response or their history) as a suggestion of kind **promotion** — no target until decided — and the `Answer` domain's owner or an Admin decides it **one at a time, never in bulk**, seeing first the proposed Answer with its trust words and one line naming the closest existing `Answer` (found when opened, under the decider's predicate, by one judging call with three labels: same · variant · different), the diff one disclosure down, with three acts — *update the existing*, *add as new*, *decline with a reason* — and client-specific wording highlighted to strip. Accepting is one governed write with the decider as author; the include markers become `sources[]` footnotes (ADR 0015); a body with a marker and no citation row is refused; the response is marked *needs review — cite the Answer*, never versioned on someone's behalf. **A trim makes the decider the generator**: `generated.by` is the last human to change the body, so a decider who edited cannot check in the same act and the `Answer` lands *Unchecked*. An **uncited answer may be promoted**: it lands `status: draft` with no sources, shown *Unchecked · no evidence*, hidden from new answers until its owner attaches evidence and moves it to stable — it cannot be checked until it has something to check against. A concept raised from unmapped passages is **drafted by the platform on request** (one extraction call, against the ceiling, written for the file), edited by the person, and accepted by the domain's owner or an Admin as an *edit* suggestion of kind *new concept*; the pipeline never prepares one unasked. **Assembled prose waits**: a changed atom marks every citing composition *needs review* at once; the scheduled guide review prepares one waiting composition rewrite per section, at most N flagged sections per run, oldest first, or one is asked for on demand with its cost said in words; a person accepts it per section. **Control Centre is six screens** — **Sources** (bindings, the two gates, the priced plan, backlog, gone-at-source impact, citation repair, agent tokens, the ceiling, the ingestion roll-up), **Suggestions** (every waiting suggestion of every kind in one queue; promotions decided singly), **Knowledge** (the review table over every concept and composition, with Conflicts and Verification requests as saved filters and exports on its toolbar), **Questions** (the answer audit as a table, flagged first, with the weekly trend in its header and the promotions and answer tests as tabs), **People** (roles, owners, thresholds per role, erasure and suppression, tokens) and **System** (the named signals, health, routes and spend, backups, repository export).

We decided this because the platform's promise is that a wrong answer can be traced to the level it went wrong at and fixed there, visibly — a thumbs-down nobody can act on is a log, and a reason that is already a record in someone's queue is the workflow; because a retrieval fix without a replayable test is a belief; because the bid library grows from use, and a gate that only Admins can feed is a gate nobody feeds, while one with no second pair of eyes turns one tender's wording into company knowledge; because a fact a person typed is the company's knowledge of it even with nothing behind it, and refusing it at the gate would refuse the very thing the first client's bid library is; because *verify* means against sources and a word that changes meaning on one rung breaks the ladder; because a Brief that rewrites itself overnight is a document nobody wrote; and because six nouns, one per thing an Admin is answerable for, are what a person holds without reading, and *Answers* on the door would send them to the bid library's wrong home.

## Considered options

- **Thinning by `UPDATE`** — rewrites a month's partition into dead tuples and holds locks; the content child and a partition drop are free.
- **90 days then deleted** (Guru) — loses the bid cycle and the test history; **keep everything until erasure** — the largest text table in the estate within a year.
- **Thumbs only, Admin triages** — the Admin as router; **free text only** — nothing routes; **"missing" as a reader's reason** — a reader cannot say which level a gap is at.
- **Promotion as an `edit` suggestion** — an edit has one target and a base hash at proposal; a promotion has neither until decided.
- **Admin-only promotion**; **every completed response promoted unless declined** (Responsive's default); **a Bid writer committing directly** — the library at the Admin's pace, full of client names, or with no second reader.
- **Refusing an uncited answer until evidence is attached** — the writer stops proposing; **"check it as a human"** on a no-evidence Answer — *verify* would mean something else on that rung.
- **The pipeline preparing a candidate concept on every unanswered question** — declined in ADR 0016 for spend and the Admin holding the clock.
- **Corrections as notes, evals later** — Guru's log; **an external eval tool** — a second system holding the company's questions; **replaying the draft** — twenty to sixty minutes and millions of tokens for nothing a test asserts; **one pass-rate percentage** — falls every time the knowledge legitimately changes.
- **Lenient generator at the gate** — one person drafts, trims and checks on one screen; convenient, and the check no longer means a second person.
- **Prose auto-refreshed**; **a rewrite on every commit** — silent change on a quoted page; a token bill per commit.
- **Four screens**; **verb-named screens** (Review · Accept · Publish · Fix · Run); **"Answers" for the audit screen**; **"sections"** — one queue split in two; the same word on the door and on the button; the wrong door; a collision with a guide's sections.

## Consequences

- `answer_audit` is a skeleton with `answer_audit_citation` rows and a month-partitioned `answer_audit_content` child; retention per workspace against shared partitions, the held-rows table, `flag_state` indexed with `at`, unguessable ids, the distinct-(person, day) aggregate, `suggestion.kind = promotion` with its payload and decision, `answer_test` with a synthetic principal and a *stale* state, the partial unique index on waiting rewrites — ticket 41's schema spike. The erasure union covers the content child, the test's question, the promotion payload and feedback notes (ticket 24).
- Feedback is given only on an answer the person received (`[SEC2]`), through tRPC, OpenAPI and an MCP feedback tool (ticket 21); one *not found* for missing and forbidden alike.
- The judging route gains a second prompt (same · variant · different) under one purpose; `prompt_version` on `llm_call` so a prompt change triggers a replay; the judge runs at temperature 0 (ticket 29).
- Signals: passed · failed · stale, replay spend beside extraction, the thinning job's lag, the Suggestions backlog counting promotions once, flags per hundred answers with last week's number, open corrections, "you copied this and it changed since" for the inbox and digest (ticket 42; who receives it, ticket 23).
- Ticket 23 carries the *no evidence* rung, the rule that reuse is neither a generation nor a check, and who may propose from history; ticket 50 the `domain_path` an `Answer` lands in; ticket 24 what a *should not have shown* report does to a binding and the strip highlighter's rules.
- On reader screens the words are *Save as an Answer*, *flag*, *helpful*, *answer test*; *promote*, *eval*, *moderation*, *rating*, *log* and *report* never appear. The platform *marks* a composition *needs review*; a reader *flags*. `CONTEXT.md` gains *screen*, *feedback*, *flag*, *correction*, *answer test*, *promotion*, *promotion gate*; *Control Centre*, *suggestion*, *answer audit* and *needs review* are edited.
- Reopens nothing in ADRs 0001–0016; applies 0012 (the accept is the governed write), 0014 (the families), 0015 (the marker rewrite) and 0016 (the walk and judge at the gate).

**Amended by ADR 0025 (28/08/2026):** the thinning job also thins heartbeats, `platform_event` and `llm_call` rows; the replay reads `llm_call`.

## Amendment — 2026-08-30, roles are levels, one mounted-transport rule, and the repository export is a System act (ticket 79, the pre-build gate; applied by T-001)

Three sweeps, none reopening this ADR.

- **Roles are levels.** ADR 0019 made them **Admin · Editor · Viewer**, never job titles, and the rename never reached this file: where this ADR says *a Bid writer saves an answer* read **an Editor**; where the rejected option says *a Bid writer committing directly* read **an Editor**.
- **One functional test per capability runs through every mounted transport.** This ADR requires a test "through tRPC, OpenAPI and MCP alike"; ADR 0008's amendment leaves the generated OpenAPI document **unmounted in v0.1**, so the rule is a transport that is actually served — tRPC, MCP and `/agent/v1` today, OpenAPI the day it mounts (`[SEC2]`).
- **The repository export is a System act, and stays there.** Control Centre's **System** screen carries the whole-repository export (`git bundle`), as this ADR already lists it; **Knowledge's** toolbar exports are *knowledge* exports — concepts, compositions and the records over them. The distinction is the minting rule (ADR 0011): a knowledge export takes what the company knows, a repository export takes the store it is kept in.

Everything else in this ADR and its amendments stands.
